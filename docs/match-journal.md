# The match journal

A pen-and-paper template for getting better at Riftbound, to be ruled up by hand in a
notebook you already own. **This is a plan, not a finished thing** — the layout below
has never been used at a table, and the open questions at the bottom are real. Nothing
in the app reads it yet.

## Why this exists, and why it is a template rather than a product

[Codex Journals](https://codexjournals.com/products/riftbound-journal-codex-of-arcane-standard-edition)
sells a Riftbound match journal: A5, spiral-bound, 188 pages, £30 — and currently sold
out. There is a [digital edition](https://codexjournals.com/products/riftbound-journal-digital-codex-of-the-arcane)
at £20, a PDF for tablets.

Neither is worth buying for what it is, because the value is entirely in the page
design and the page design is guessable. What is *not* guessable from their listing is
the detail, so this records what could actually be established about it rather than
what it might contain.

## What their journal contains, as far as it can be established

**They publish no interior previews**, for any of their titles, and no third-party
unboxing shows a page. The only hint at density is a customer review: *"Each page covers
way more info than I expected."*

But the same journal is sold for three games, and each listing describes its own
version. The differences give away the template:

| | Flesh and Blood | Lorcana | Riftbound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tracker ×80** | Life Tracker | Lore Tracking | Point Tracker |
| **Review ×80** | key plays and decisions | glimmers played, inkwell decisions, win/loss, reflections | battlefield choices, point progression, key reflections |
| **Milestone ×8** | deck performance, mental game, next steps | deck performance, meta position | deck synergy, matchups, performance |
| **Notes** | deck tweaks, sideboarding | theorycrafting | tech swaps, event observations |

So the structure is: a **tracker page filled in live**, carrying whatever the game's
running score resource is; a **review page filled in after**, carrying that game's
characteristic decisions plus win/loss and a reflection; a **milestone every ten
matches**; and notes at the back.

**The one thing worth stealing is the milestone cadence.** Reviewing every ten games
rather than every game is what turns a pile of logs into a decision about the deck.

## What it is for

**Improving, not record-keeping.** A scoreboard says *how* a game was lost; only your
own notes say *why*, and the why is the part you can change. So every page is built
around three questions — where did it turn, what did I do wrong, what will I do
instead — and the ten-game review exists to find the mistakes that **repeat**. One
mistake in ten matches is noise. The same one three times is a habit, and habits are what
this is for.

**Write as little as possible at the table.** Anything that takes more than a few seconds
mid-game will not get written on round three of a long night, and a page that half-fills
stops being used. So every field belongs to **the moment you actually have time for it**
— during a game, between games, after the match, or at home — and the during-game part
is only what you would otherwise forget.

**The unit is the match, not the game.** Nexus Night is best of three, so a page is one
match.

## What you are allowed to write, and when

Nexus Night is low OPL (TR §205.2, §601.2.d.2.a), and the note rules apply there with no
exception — see [Tournament rules worth carrying](rules.md#tournament-rules-worth-carrying):

- **During a game, only this match's page.** You may write notes and use them in the
  same match (TR §416.1), but you may **not** refer to anything from outside it —
  previous pages, the trouble list, the shorthand key — while a game is on (TR §416.4).
  **Close the notebook to the current page before each game.**
- **Between games, anything.** Notes made before the match are fair game, privately
  (TR §416.5). This is when to read the trouble list — it is also when you sideboard.
- **The page starts empty and visible** to your opponent (TR §416.2). You don't have to
  show them what you write afterwards (TR §416.3).
- **A pre-printed template**: the rules don't say whether printed blank fields count as
  "empty". They carry no strategy, so it should be fine — but **show it to the judge
  once** rather than assume. What a template must never carry is *content*: no old
  "next time I'll" reminders, no trouble list, no shorthand key. Those are notes from
  outside the match.
- **A tablet or e-ink device** (Kindle Scribe, iPad) may replace paper at low OPL
  (TR §417.2), at the head judge's discretion (TR §417.3) — otherwise devices are out
  during matches (TR §417.1). Ask once. If allowed, open a fresh page *before* the match
  and do not navigate away during games: everything else on the device is outside the
  match.

## What Bo3 changes

These are decisions that only exist across games, so the page records them:

- **Who plays first.** For game 1 a designated player, chosen by any agreed random
  method, decides (TR §407.1, §407.2). After that **the loser of the previous game
  chooses**; after a draw the previous order stands (TR §407.4). Going second channels
  an extra rune on your first Channel Phase (§485.7), so the choice is real.
- **Which battlefield.** Each game you choose one of your three, and a battlefield used in
  a decided game is retired for the match (§486.6). Leading with your best one, or saving
  it, is a decision worth checking afterwards.
- **Sideboarding**, if your store uses it. Not before game 1 (TR §403.5); between games,
  1-for-1 (TR §403.4), and you may change your Chosen Champion (TR §403.4.a). Game 1 is
  therefore always your main deck against theirs, and games 2–3 are both players'
  adjusted lists — the review page splits them for that reason.

## Shorthand

Learn these rather than writing them on the page — a key on the sheet is a note from
outside the match. Every mid-game note is **turn number + one symbol + two or three
words**.

| mark | means | example |
|---|---|---|
| `!` | I hesitated — revisit this decision | `4! hold back?` |
| `x` | I knew it was a mistake as I made it | `6x attacked into 3 open` |
| `T` | trouble card, and what it did | `5T Rocket — killed my 5` |
| `s` | surprised me | `3s react on my move` |
| `?` | rules question to look up | `7? Hidden + Star Spring` |
| `d` | my Conquer drew instead of scoring (missed the 7+ gate, §471.1.b) | `8d` |

For the slots between games:

| mark | means |
|---|---|
| `1` / `2` | I went first / second |
| `c` | ...and I chose it (e.g. `2c` — I chose to go second) |
| `W` `L` `D` | won / lost / drew |
| `m0` `m1` `m2` | mulliganed 0, 1 or 2 cards (§117) |
| `+Card −Card` | sideboard swap, 1-for-1 |
| `@7` | it was decided on turn 7 |

**Cards:** first word or first four letters — `Rock` (Super Mega Death Rocket!), `Swit`, `Kha`. Consistent beats
readable; you are the only reader. **A card you can't name:** write what it did and the
art, `5T ?blue dragon — stole hold`, and look it up at home.

## The match page

```
#__ date ______ event ____________ rd __  list v__          match W/L  __–__
me  ______________ / ______________
opp ______________ / ______________

    first   BF mine / theirs           mull   sideboard          result
G1  ____    _________ / _________      ____   —                  ___ @__
G2  ____    _________ / _________      ____   ______________     ___ @__
G3  ____    _________ / _________      ____   ______________     ___ @__

notes ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
G1 ________________________________________________________________________
   ________________________________________________________________________
G2 ________________________________________________________________________
   ________________________________________________________________________
G3 ________________________________________________________________________
   ________________________________________________________________________

after ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
my mistake      ______________________________  type _   (wins too)
next time I'll  ____________________________________
```

**That is everything you write at the table.** The rest — the full reflection, the
trouble list, the review — happens at home, from these notes.

### During a game

Only the **notes** lines, and only when something happens: turn + mark + a few words.
Most turns you write nothing. A typical game's notes read:
`3s react on my move · 4! hold back? · 5T Rock — killed my 5 · 7? Hidden + move`.

Score is not on the page — track it with dice or an app as you already do. `@turn` in the
result slot is enough to see when it was decided.

### Between games

Fill that game's row: `first`, battlefields, mull, result and `@turn`. Then, if you lost,
choose first or last; pick your battlefield; sideboard and write the swaps in the next
row. **This is also when you may open the trouble list** (TR §416.5). About thirty
seconds, and you are sideboarding anyway.

### After the match

Two lines, **before the next round** — within an hour you will remember the result and
very little of the decision behind it. Read the `!` and `x` marks first; the mistake is
usually one of them.

- **`my mistake`** — one, the biggest, across the whole match. Your decision, not their
  draw. **Wins get this line too**: an opponent's errors hide yours, and a win with no
  mistake noted is the most suspicious entry on the page.
- **`type`** — one letter for what *kind* of mistake it was, from
  [strategy.md §8](strategy.md#8-reviewing-a-game): **`t`**erminal (missed a win or a
  loss to prevent), **`s`**tate, **`o`**bjective, **`c`**onversion, **`i`**nformation,
  **`p`**ressure, **`r`**ole. The words in `my mistake` change from match to match; the
  letter is what lets the review page see the same mistake three times.
- **`next time I'll`** — the mistake rewritten as a rule you could follow at the table.
  "Next time I'll count their open runes before attacking", not "play better".

### At home

With the notes in front of you:

- **Copy every `T`** to the [trouble list](#the-trouble-list-at-the-back) — a new row, or a
  tally mark on an existing one.
- **Settle every `?`** against [`docs/rules.md`](rules.md). One that needed several rules
  composed to answer belongs in [`docs/rules-rulings.md`](rules-rulings.md).
- **Look up any card you couldn't name**, and read the actual text of every trouble card
  — half of "I can't beat that card" is a misread of what it does.
- **Answer the `!` flags you still can.** The ones you can't go on the review page. For
  each, run the terminal scan first (was there a win, or a loss to stop?). Then write the
  two lines you were choosing between, and for each the clean, awkward and punish reply
  ([strategy.md §0](strategy.md#0-the-order-of-every-decision)). Don't just start
  from the turn the game was lost. Find the first turn where your options got
  narrower than theirs.
- **Ask about your opponent's view** if you got the chance at the table — "what would you
  have done on turn 4?" is often better review than your own notes. Jot it beside the
  match.

## The review page, every ten matches

```
matches __–__   deck ____________   lists v__–v__   matches __–__   games __–__

  vs                 match  games   why
  _______________    W/L    _–_     ___________________
  _______________    W/L    _–_     ___________________
  _______________    W/L    _–_     ___________________
  _______________    W/L    _–_     ___________________

game 1 (main deck)  __–__        games 2–3 (sided)  __–__
going first         __–__        going second       __–__

mistakes that repeated   ______________________________
                         ______________________________
by type   t __  s __  o __  c __  i __  p __  r __
"next time I'll" I kept  ______________________________
"next time I'll" I broke ______________________________
flags I still can't answer ____________________________

worst matchup __________  → plan ______________________
top trouble card ________  → answer ____________________
sideboard cards that earned it _________________________
battlefield I should lead with _________________________
card I kept wanting       ______________________________
card that did nothing     ______________________________
mulligans I'd change      ______________________________

one change to my play  _________________________________
one change to my deck  _________________________________
```

- **`mistakes that repeated`** is the payoff. Read the `my mistake` lines together and look
  for the same thing in different words.
- **`by type`** tallies the ten `type` letters. A column with three or more is the habit;
  the matching section of [strategy.md](strategy.md) is what to reread before the next
  block. A `t` is worth fixing first: it is the only type where the right play was
  already on the board.
- **`kept` / `broke`** checks whether last block's lessons actually changed anything. A
  rule you keep breaking needs to be simpler, not repeated.
- **`game 1` vs `games 2–3`** is the Bo3 split. Strong game 1s and weak sided games point at
  the sideboard plan; the reverse points at the main deck.
- **One change each to play and deck**, and only one — and **change the list only here**,
  not mid-block. A list that changes after match 4 turns the review into two half-reviews
  of two decks. `list v__` on each page is the record if you break this.
- **Ten matches find habits, not win rates.** Three matches against one archetype says
  almost nothing about the matchup. Use this page to fix how you play, and be slow to
  change the deck on its numbers.
- **`top trouble card`** comes straight off the trouble list: the one with the most marks
  and no answer that works yet.
- **`card I kept wanting`** is how a real conclusion about a deck arrives from play rather
  than from simulation — the second Switcheroo in
  [the midrange dossier](../guides/khazix-voidreaver-postban.md) is exactly the kind of
  finding that should have come from ten matches rather than from a Monte Carlo.

## The trouble list, at the back

A running page — or a few — at the back of the notebook, **kept across every deck and
every night**. Every `T` on a match page feeds it. **Read it between games, never during
one** (TR §416.4, §416.5) — between games is when you sideboard, so that is when it is
useful anyway.

```
card              their legend     marks     what it does to me      answer            worked?
______________    ____________     ||||      __________________      ______________    y/n/?
______________    ____________     ||        __________________      ______________    y/n/?
______________    ____________     |         __________________      ______________    y/n/?
```

- **One row per card, a mark each time it hurts you.** First time it appears, add a row;
  after that, just add a tally mark. The marks are the point — the card at the top of the
  list after a month is not necessarily the best card in the format, it is the one *you*
  have the worst answer to.
- **`what it does to me`** — the effect in your terms, not its rules text: "removes my
  only threat", "out-holds me on their battlefield". Two different cards that do the same
  thing to you are the same problem, and usually the same fix.
- **`answer`** is one of three kinds, and it helps to say which:
  - **a card** — something in your main deck or sideboard that deals with it;
  - **a play pattern** — how to play around it: what to hold back, what not to commit
    into, when to make them use it early;
  - **none yet** — write that down honestly. A trouble card with no answer is the most
    useful row on the page; it is a question to bring back here.
- **`worked?`** — filled in the next time you meet it. An answer that has not been
  tested is a guess.
- **Look the card up afterwards** before deciding the answer. Read its actual text, and
  how the rules treat it, in [`docs/rules.md`](rules.md). Half of "I can't beat that
  card" turns out to be a misread of what it does.

## On a Kindle Scribe

The same pages as PDFs you write on with the pen. **Ask the store's judge first** — a
device may replace paper at low OPL only at the head judge's discretion (TR §417.2,
§417.3).

**Keep three separate files**, because only one of them may be open during a game
(TR §416.4):

| file | when it may be open |
|---|---|
| `match-night.pdf` — five blank match pages | during games: **only the current match's page** |
| `trouble-list.pdf` | between games and at home |
| `review.pdf` | at home |

**Building them:** `scripts/build-journal` writes all three to `guides/out/journal/`
(`--pages N` for more match pages). They are 6 × 8 in, the Scribe's 3:4, and print fine
scaled to A4 or Letter.

**Getting them on: Send to Kindle, not USB.** Send each PDF with Amazon's Send to Kindle
(the web page, the app, or email to your Kindle address). That converts it to a format
the pen can write on; a PDF copied over USB opens but reportedly takes no pen strokes.
Sent PDFs land in the Library, not in Notebooks, and the Scribe has no custom notebook
templates — so the PDF itself is the template, and a fresh copy is a fresh send.

**Each Nexus Night, send a fresh `match-night.pdf`.** One page per match. Before each
match, open the next blank page and show it to your opponent (TR §416.2); during games,
don't flip back to earlier rounds — they are notes from previous matches (TR §416.4).
Flipping back between games is fine (TR §416.5).

## Open questions

1. **Should the trouble list split by deck?** Kept as one list for now: a card that
   beats one of your decks often beats the others for the same reason.
2. **Do the notes lines get used under time pressure?** They are the field most likely
   to be skipped in a close game, and the one that matters most when it is.
3. **Is a printed template "empty" under TR §416.2?** Not stated. Ask the store's judge,
   and record the answer here.
4. **Kindle Scribe template** — see [On a Kindle Scribe](#on-a-kindle-scribe); not yet
   tried at a table, and device use at the store needs the judge's yes (TR §417.2,
   §417.3).
5. **Should the app read any of this back?** Win-rate by matchup would feed
   `scripts/rift`, but ten-match blocks are too small to mean much and it means typing
   matches in twice. Deliberately deferred. The trouble list is the likeliest exception —
   it is short, and "what beats me" would sharpen deck advice.
