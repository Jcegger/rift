# Deckbuilding sources (read 21 Nov 2026)

What each source says, in its own words where it matters, and whether our data can check it. The principles
drawn from these are in [deckbuilding-theory.md](deckbuilding-theory.md); the measurements are in
[deckbuilding.md](deckbuilding.md).

## Riftbound

**Riot, "Deckbuilding Primer"**, Dave Guskin (Game Director), 28 Jul 2025.
[playriftbound.com](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/deckbuilding-primer/)
- The legend's always-on ability should shape the strategy. Signature: up to 3, "you should have a good reason not to" play the max.
- "Play 9+ small units" (2–4 cost), so one is drawn by turn 1 or 2. "Play 6+ interactive spells." "When in doubt, play more units."
- Runes: start 6–6; skew (e.g. 8–4) toward the domain with the heavier Power costs.
- "Play more of your champion." **Data disagrees:** 93% of Radiance good finishes run exactly one champion unit.
- Build a rough draft, playtest, iterate.

**riftbound.gg, "Riftbound TCG Deck Archetypes: How to Find Your Playstyle"**, razviar, updated 20 Jul 2026.
[link](https://riftbound.gg/riftbound-tcg-deck-archetypes-how-to-find-your-playstyle/)
- Aggro, control, combo; the win condition is holding battlefields. "Stick with one deck long enough to learn not
  just how it wins, but also why it loses."

**riftboundguide.com, "Deck Tips"** (undated, Unleashed era). [link](https://riftboundguide.com/deck-tips/)
- 3 copies of a good card, 1–2 of a situational one; "plenty of 1 to 3 Energy cards"; "at least 20 Unit cards."
  Beginner advice: winners run 19.4 units.

**riftbound.gg RQ reports**, Den: [Los Angeles](https://riftbound.gg/los-angeles-regional-qualifier-results-top-8-decks-metagame-popularity-win-conversion-rates/)
(29 Sep 2026), [Hartford](https://riftbound.gg/hartford-regional-qualifier/) (22 Jun 2026).
- Track **conversion** (share of a legend's players who make the cut), not just popularity: Kennen led Day 1
  conversion in LA and then didn't survive the top 8. Hartford: a new combo Lux reached the quarterfinals.
- Card-choice reasoning isn't in any written source found. Winner interviews exist only as video (Factor, Hartford
  RQ, Master Yi; Gorica, Singapore RQ, Akali "almost nobody would touch"). Not read.

Not readable from here: Mobalytics' deckbuilding fundamentals, riftdecks.com's win-rate tier list (both 403),
TCGplayer's LA RQ article (empty page).

## Magic: The Gathering

**Patrick Chapin, "Next Level Magic Preview: Bottom-Up Thinking"**, StarCityGames, 22 May 2009.
[link](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/next-level-magic-preview-how-to-use-bottom-up-thinking-to-improve-deck-building-and-qualify/)
- "Top-Down thinking is when you look to see what is there. Bottom-Up thinking is looking to see what is not there."
  Start top-down, then bottom-up.
- A per-format checklist: the dominant creatures, the cheap removal, how people get card advantage, what
  countermagic exists. "The non-existence of cards in those categories is just as important."
- Cuts: "find cards that cost too much for what they do"; after an event, "which cards shone?" and "were you really
  using the right card for the job?"
- Build front to back ("What would I like to have happen on turn 1?"): "many people design weak decks because they
  can only imagine best-case scenarios."

**Patrick Chapin, "Metagaming and Deckbuilding: The Three Laws of Prediction"**, StarCityGames, 13 Jul 2010.
[link](https://articles.starcitygames.com/?p=17166)
- "People don't show up with 'random' decks", so the field can be predicted. "Try to beat that, not just join it."
- For a new deck: "Do something worth doing." Ask what a linear strategy "is paying you"; a combo, "is it worth doing
  instead of something else?"; midrange: "make sure your card quality is top-notch."

**Chapin, Next Level Deckbuilding previews** (StarCityGames, Jul–Aug 2013): card evaluation by "rate" and
"contextual strengths"; mana bases. Teaching content is in images; only the topic lists were readable.

**Frank Karsten, "How Many Lands Do You Need to Consistently Hit Your Land Drops?"**, 30 May 2017 (PDF copy).
- Method: fix a mulligan strategy, compute hypergeometric odds of hitting each land drop on the play and the draw,
  then check against a regression on top-performing decks. Example: 24 lands in 60 hit the 4th land drop by turn
  4 80.6% on the draw, 71.3% on the play. The method transfers; the numbers don't (Riftbound has no lands).

**Reid Duke, "Building a Mana Base"**, Wizards, 24 Nov 2014.
[link](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/building-mana-base-2014-11-24)
- "Building a mana base is the most neglected aspect of deck building." "You cannot know what's possible and what's
  impossible before you have your mana base." Build the spells and the mana together.

**Mike Flores, "Who's the Beatdown?"** (1999). Not found online in this session; summarised from memory, so it's
labelled that way wherever it's used: in any matchup one deck must be the aggressor and one the controller, and
"misassignment of role = loss."

## Other games

**Frank Karsten, "Building a Winning Disney Lorcana Deck" (10 tips)**, disneylorcana.com, 18 Sep 2025.
[link](https://www.disneylorcana.com/en-US/news/2025/09/2025-9-10_deck_building_tips)
- Copies: the max for essentials, one fewer for "diminishing returns" cards, 2 for curve splits, 1 for toolbox singletons.
- "Rule of Eight": about 8 functionally similar cards gives about 65% to have one in the opening 7 of 60.
- "Without at least four copies of either pillar [card advantage, removal], your deck is unbalanced."
- Tech: pick cards "great in every matchup, on both play and draw"; check the metagame before adding answers.
- Deck size: stay at the minimum; an extra card "can be no better than the best" minimum-size deck.

**Snap.fan, "Top Ten Features Overlooked When Deck Building"** (Marvel Snap), 5 May 2026.
[link](https://snap.fan/news/top-ten-features-overlooked-when-deck-building/)
- "Strong decks have plays that they can reliably make on turns 1-3."
- "You Can't Tech For Everything": about 2 flex slots; over-teching harms the game plan.
- Testing: "give something 10 games before scrapping an idea."

**Herko Kerghans, "LoR Ladder Update, Off-Meta Edition"** (Legends of Runeterra), 21 Jun 2022.
[link](https://riwan.substack.com/p/lor-off-meta-decks-220621)
- Brews judged on win rate with the exact list: "Should Work" needs at least 100 games; 40–80 games is "Proceed with
  Care". Warns that "a single highly-skilled (and/or extremely-lucky) player could explain" a brew's results.

Not readable from here: Flesh and Blood's pitch-ratio articles (dead host, 404), PokéBeach's "Knowing the Odds" (403).
