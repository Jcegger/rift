## 1. Identity — carried over from v1 (agreed)

**Legend** Battle Mistress (Body/Chaos) · **Champion** Sivir, Mercenary · **Archetype**
Midrange · Extra resources (`docs/archetypes.md`): Gold into card advantage, Defy-proof tricks,
outlast the opponent. **The goal** (Jay, 26 Oct): fun, a little off-meta, 2-1 at Nexus Night.
Sivir is the favourite champion; no control ("feels mean"), no swarm, never Aurora.

**Why a v2** (28 Oct 2026). Job by job, v1 runs eight jobs, two of them on one or two
copies (Vex, the lock, 2; Evelynn, opening their battlefield, 1): by turn 5 a 2-of shows up
about 41% of the time and a 1-of about 23% (guide §4), so the lines built on them are luck.
And it has the lightest top end of any winning midrange deck looked at (two Rengar and
Sivir, nothing at 7+ Energy), which is the structural reason Riven's late game outsizes it.

## 2. How it wins — carried over, to revisit after stage 3

v1's plan (guide, executive summary): take battlefields early with cheap bodies that don't
fold; lock one; open the other; conquer for the eighth point; every enemy death readies the
legend for another Gold. **v2 adds a late game:** from turn 5, when Energy stops being the
limit (guide §4), threats that outsize the board. Cheap in Energy on turns 1–4, the spending
from turn 5: the identity rule, sharpened rather than broken.

**v2: four jobs and two rules** (Jay: "max six jobs… should we go even lower?"):

| job | what it covers | target |
|---|---|---|
| **Bodies** | units that take battlefields **and** carry value: draw, recur, Gold | ~18 |
| **Fight tricks** | Defy-proof pumps and swaps | ~7 |
| **Interaction pool** | kill, bounce, stun, pull, strip: whatever is in the way. Vex, Evelynn, Rebuke, Rampage, Star-Crossed and Sabotage would all sit here, so no line needs one card | ~9 |
| **Top end** | threats that outsize the board from turn 5 | ~6 (Sivir counts) |

- **Curve rule:** 8+ cards at 2 Energy or less, so turns 1–3 work.
- **Identity rule:** cheap in Energy on turns 1–4; the expensive cards are the top end. Gold is
  the resource, not a job.

**Why four and not fewer:** each does something the others can't (a trick for the fight is not
an answer for the unit). Midrange's flexibility lives in the interaction pool being broad
(several kinds of answer), not in having many jobs. **The cost:** with four jobs every card
has to be good at its job; there are no slots for "nice to have".

**Agreed** (Jay left both to Claude: "up to what you think would be the best choice", and

### The four jobs, named for Sivir's engine, with a spec each (28 Oct 2026)

*Jay: "we should be specific about each card… these are a bit generic vs the labels you gave
to other decks." The winners' jobs are named for how that deck does it ("bodies that pay off
when they die", "arriving where it hurts"); these are named for Gold and the trash. **The spec
is the test a card must pass to earn a slot in stage 4.***

| # | job | spec | v1 now | target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trash-and-recursion bodies**: take a battlefield, leave something behind | 3 Energy or less and 1 Power or less (cheap, and Matriarch can revive it); **leaves value whether it lives or dies** (a card, a Gold, a choice in the trash), **or fights above its cost** (Staunch Survivor: 4 Might attacking or defending, for 2 Energy): the deck's defence against removal is replacing units, not saving them | Merchant 3, Ezreal 3, Fizz 3, Innovator 3, Matriarch 3, Tornado 2, Staunch 2 (19) | 17 |
| 2 | **Gold-paid fight tricks**: win the fight with Power, where Defy can't reach, **or save the few units that must stay** | 2 Energy or less; paid in Power; usable in the fight (Action, Reaction or from face down); out of Defy's reach (2+ Power, or it chooses your own unit). **Plus 1–2 saves** for Vex, Matriarch and the top end: Star-Crossed first (it saves and answers at once), Repulse for a targeted spell; Defy can stop either, accepted | Punch First 3, Switcheroo 2, Edge of Night 2 (7) | 7 |
| 3 | **Clear the battlefield you need**: break their hold, stop theirs (how the deck scores: hold one, take the other) | a **mix of kinds** (kill, bounce, stun, pull, strip) so there is an answer to every threat type; **half or more works through Zhonya's** (bounce, stun, pull, strip), since kills are blunted in the Calm decks at the top; Power-paid preferred (Defy-proof); spells Fizz can recast (3 Energy or less); **1–2 may be sweepers** | kill: Rampage 3 · strip: Sabotage 2 · bounce: Star-Crossed 2, Rebuke 1 · lock: Vex 2 · pull: Evelynn 1 (11) | 10 |
| 4 | **Turn-5 threats that spend the spare Power**: outsize the board once Energy stops being tight | 5+ Energy or 6+ Might; out of Gust's range (4+ Might); **value when it lands**, since bounce is in nearly every top list; ideally a sink for spare Gold; **sweeping when it attacks or defends is preferred** (the field is small units, below) | Rengar, Trophy Hunter 2, Sivir, Mercenary (3) | 6 |

**Checked against the meta** (28 Oct 2026; Jay: "are those the best goals against the meta?",
and "keep in mind what is within purple and orange… we may not have a true save-a-unit option"):
- **Job 1:** the field's damage removal is bigger than pings: Falling Star (3, twice) in 100% of
  Kai'Sa's and Akali's Radiance results, Hextech Ray 3, Thousand-Tailed Watcher −3 (100% of
  Kai'Sa's and LeBlanc's). A 3-Might body survives none of it, so the spec asks for value on
  arrival or death instead.
- **Job 3:** Zhonya's is in Riven 93%, Akali 100%, Master Yi (Wuju Bladesman) 100%: half the pool
  must not depend on a kill. Rampage stays for the other half of the field (Kennen, Kai'Sa,
  Rengar and LeBlanc run no Zhonya's).
- **Job 2:** most of the meta's removal is targeted, and every winning midrange list protects its
  key unit (Riven: Defy, Discipline, Zhonya's; Fiora: Repulse, Riposte; Sett: Repulse). **Body and
  Chaos have no Defy-proof save**: Star-Crossed (own unit to hand, theirs too), Repulse, Unyielding
  Spirit (damage only), Abandon (spells only) are all Defy-able. So the deck replaces rather than
  protects, and the 1–2 saves are only for the units whose job is to stay: Vex (which has Deflect),
  Matriarch before she revives, the top end.
- **Sweepers, measured (Jay: "look at A tier decks too"):** the S and A decks are full of small
  units, not tokens. Units at 4 Might or less, of all units: Rengar 26/30, Evelynn 22/25, Kennen
  18/21, Riven 15/20, Vex 15/19, Irelia 13/13, Kai'Sa 14/21, Master Yi 11/16, LeBlanc 14/31 (17
  big); Azir runs 7 Sand Soldier makers; Jayce almost no units. A one-battlefield sweep is often
  a 2-for-1 against 9 of the 11. **Built in as a preference inside two jobs, not a fifth job**:
  top end that sweeps (Graves, Blasting Through; Anivia, Primal) and 1–2 sweepers in the pool
  (Bullet Time; Cannon Barrage). Leads for stage 4, each still to pass its spec.
- **No fifth job:** a sweeper job of its own is not needed once the sweepers ride in the top end and
  the pool (above).

**What the specs show before stage 4:**
- **Job 4's spec is strict** ("value when it lands"): Rengar's Ambush is surprise, not value, so
  he meets it half way. The job to search for.
- ~~Staunch Survivor fails job 1's spec~~ **corrected** (Jay: "Staunch is really a 4 Might unit"): he
  fights above his cost, 4 Might attacking or defending for 2 Energy, holding battlefields in a
  field of small attackers, and he is the card the identity rule was written from (back in on
  26 Oct). The spec now accepts "fights above its cost". Weak only outside a fight (2 Might: Gust,
  3-damage removal). The bodies that go come out of the jobs 1–3 read, not a first guess.
- **Job 3's mix is lopsided**: kill and bounce are 6 copies, lock 2, pull 1. Spreading the kinds
  points at more Rebuke or Vex, not more Rampage.

**The candidates** (Body or Chaos, legal, not Aurora; spec in §3):

| card | cost | spec | the read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beast Below (Chaos) | 7E 2P, 8 Might | fully | when played, returns another friendly unit and an enemy unit to hand: bounces their threat, replays your Ezreal or Matriarch. Unproven (12 lists, 3 recorded) |
| Baron Nashor (Chaos) | 10E 3P, 12 Might | fully | can't be chosen; others +2; a 3-Power Gold sink. 101 recorded results (Riven). **Out: see the rune board below** |
| Rengar, Trophy Hunter (Body) | 5E 1P, 6 Might | half | Ambush; 141 recorded; the winners run 3 |
| Graves, Blasting Through (Body) | 5E 1P, 4 Might | half | the sweeper: 4 to every enemy unit at his battlefield on attack or defence (9 with Punch First); 46 recorded |
| Ocean Drake (Chaos) | 8E 2P, 7 Might | fully | can be played onto an open battlefield; optional bounce on play |

**The rune board caps the top end at about 7 Energy** (Jay: "I'm worried about energy production,
because I still need to generate Gold"). The legend's Gold comes from recycling runes, which
keeps the board small. lib/sim.mjs engine games, normal pressure, 6,000 a deck, the v2 candidate
(−2 Staunch, −1 Rampage, +Beast Below, +Graves, +Baron):

| runes on the board | turn 4 | turn 5 | turn 6 | turn 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| average | 6.5 | 7.3 | 7.8 | 9.0 |
| 5+ (Rengar, Graves) | 94% | 95% | 96% | 98% |
| 7+ (Beast Below, Anivia) | 54% | 71% | 78% | 90% |
| 8+ (Ocean Drake) | 22% | 48% | 58% | 79% |
| **10+ (Baron)** | **0%** | **7%** | **17%** | **42%** |

v1 is the same within a point. **Baron is out**: a 10-cost card fights the legend; Riven casts
him because Astral Heron discounts him. **Gold does not suffer from a bigger top end**: 4.18 → 4.32
Gold a game, because expensive cards make the deck recycle more; Energy on turns 1–4 is unchanged
(15.3 → 15.2). The cost is a slightly slower board (units by turn 5: 4.9 → 4.6) and 14 → 12 cheap
cards. *Caveat: the model recycles whenever it needs Power; a player saving runes for a big turn
does somewhat better, but the tension is structural.*

**Why is Beast Below so rarely played?** (Jay: "it's possible we're uncovering something, because
there's selection bias.") Claude's readings, reasoned, not tested:
1. **Returning your own unit is a drawback for most decks**, an upside only when that unit does
   something on arrival. Most units don't; this deck's do (Ezreal draws 2 again, Fizz recasts,
   Matriarch revives again). Weak for nearly everyone, good for a deck shaped like this one: a
   card that rewards an unusual shape rarely gets found by netdecking. The selection bias is real.
2. **The 7-drop slot is crowded where Chaos is good**: Riven has Heron and Baron, Kennen has Rhasa.
3. **It is tempo, not card advantage**: one bounce each is card-neutral, unless reason 1 applies.
4. **Slow and readable**: a main-phase 7-drop, no Ambush or Hidden. Not So Fast can counter its
   bounce (it chooses their unit); Defy cannot (an ability, not a spell).
5. **An older set** (Spiritforged): perhaps never tried in a Radiance shell.

**Rules question, settled (Jay; checked in the verbatim rules):** with no other friendly unit, **no
bounce at all**. "In order to put a spell or ability on the chain, valid choices must be made for all
targets" (§355.8); the trigger targets both units, the same as Star-Crossed. A small cost here: a
midrange deck nearly always has another unit by turns 5–7, and the text sets no location, so it can
be one in your base (an Ezreal you want to replay). It fails only after a wiped board. Stays on the
shortlist.

**Beast Below and Star-Crossed** (Jay: "a big unit can also get Star-Crossed, but might still be worth
it"): it will be (Kennen 100%, Evelynn 100%, Vex 100%, Riven 89% of results). But its value has
already happened by then; replaying it repeats the value; Repulse counters the Star-Crossed (it
chooses Beast and no other friendly unit). The cost is tempo: 7 Energy against their 3 and 1 Power.
Worth it, because of the arrival value: the spec doing its job.

**Graves over Anivia** (Jay: "suspicious about Anivia vs Graves, which can be stronger with Punch
First and is cheaper"): Graves is 5E 1P (castable 95% from turn 4) against 7E 2P (71% on turn 5);
sweeps attacking **or defending**, against attacking only; scales with Punch First (his blast equals
his Might: 9), against a fixed 3. Anivia's one edge: she also deals 8 combat damage, so she can clear
small units and kill a big defender in one attack, which Graves can't (no combat damage). The deck
wins by holding at 7, and Graves defending a hold is that plan. **Anivia off the shortlist**, a fallback
only if Beast Below fails.

**Job 4, agreed** (Jay: "I'd probably lean on 2 Rengars"): **Sivir, Mercenary · Rengar, Trophy Hunter ×2 ·
Graves, Blasting Through ×2 · Beast Below ×1** = 6, the stage-3 target. Two Rengars on the merits
too: Graves shares the 5-Energy slot, so two of each is already four 5-drops, and a third Rengar
would make five, competing with the turn-4 Vex and Matriarch. The winners run three because their
top end *is* Rengar. **Not too top heavy:** average about 3.0 (v1 2.77; with Baron 3.15), like Riven
and Kennen, well under LeBlanc's 4.1; the model's cost was units by turn 5 4.9 → 4.6, cheap cards 14 → 12.
