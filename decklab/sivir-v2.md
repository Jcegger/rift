## 1. Identity — carried over from v1 (agreed)

**Legend** Battle Mistress (Body/Chaos) · **Champion** Sivir, Mercenary · **Archetype**
Midrange · Extra resources (`docs/archetypes.md`): Gold into card advantage, Defy-proof tricks,
outlast the opponent. **The goal** (Jay, 26 Oct): fun, a little off-meta, 2-1 at Nexus Night.
Sivir is the favourite champion; no control ("feels mean"), no swarm, never Aurora.

**Why a v2** (28 Oct 2026). Job by job, v1 runs eight jobs, two of them on one or two
copies (Vex, the lock, 2; Evelynn, opening their battlefield, 1): by turn 5 a 2-of shows up
about 41% of the time and a 1-of about 23% (guide §4), so the lines built on them are luck.
And it has the lightest top end of any winning midrange deck looked at (two Rengar and
Sivir, nothing at 7+ Energy), which is the structural reason Riven's late game outsizes it.

## 2. How it wins — carried over, to revisit after stage 3

v1's plan (guide, executive summary): take battlefields early with cheap bodies that don't
fold; lock one; open the other; conquer for the eighth point; every enemy death readies the
legend for another Gold. **v2 adds a late game:** from turn 5, when Energy stops being the
limit (guide §4), threats that outsize the board. Cheap in Energy on turns 1–4, the spending
from turn 5: the identity rule, sharpened rather than broken.

**What the winners share** (28 Oct 2026; Riot's top decks and the LDXP Sacramento 25k):

|---|---|---|---|
| Rengar, Pridestalker — 1st, LA RQ (1,962) | ~4 | bodies, 30 | Noxus Hopeful 3, Darius, Ferrous Forerunner |
| Kennen, Heart of the Tempest — 2nd, Singapore | ~5 | movers, 13 | Rhasa 3, Nocturne 3 |
| Riven, The Exile — 14th, 25k (755) | ~5 | cheap bodies, 13 | Astral Heron 3, Baron 2, Blitzcrank 3 |
| LeBlanc, Deceiver — 50th, 25k | ~4 | death-value bodies, 16 | 11 big cards |
| Sett, The Boss — 3rd, Utrecht | ~5 | buff makers 13, bodies 13 | Rengar, Trophy Hunter 3, Fiora, Victorious 3 |
| Fiora, Grand Duelist — 8th, Singapore (*Aurora*, field analysis only) | ~4 | top end, 17 | — |

Every job 6+ deep; one-ofs only inside pools; removal often rides on bodies; real top end in
every list; and the Body toolbox they agree on: Punch First 3, Sabotage 3, Rengar, Trophy
Hunter 3, Rampage 2–3, Repulse 1–2, Challenge 2.

**v2: four jobs and two rules** (Jay: "max six jobs… should we go even lower?"):

| job | what it covers | target |
|---|---|---|
| **Bodies** | units that take battlefields **and** carry value: draw, recur, Gold | ~18 |
| **Fight tricks** | Defy-proof pumps and swaps | ~7 |
| **Interaction pool** | kill, bounce, stun, pull, strip: whatever is in the way. Vex, Evelynn, Rebuke, Rampage, Star-Crossed and Sabotage would all sit here, so no line needs one card | ~9 |
| **Top end** | threats that outsize the board from turn 5 | ~6 (Sivir counts) |

- **Curve rule:** 8+ cards at 2 Energy or less, so turns 1–3 work.
- **Identity rule:** cheap in Energy on turns 1–4; the expensive cards are the top end. Gold is
  the resource, not a job.

**Why four and not fewer:** each does something the others can't (a trick for the fight is not
an answer for the unit). Midrange's flexibility lives in the interaction pool being broad
(several kinds of answer), not in having many jobs. **The cost:** with four jobs every card
has to be good at its job; there are no slots for "nice to have".

**Agreed** (Jay left both to Claude: "up to what you think would be the best choice", and
"compare that to the data, mixed with your opinion… and the general benefits of Sivir"):

1. **Four jobs: Sabotage goes in the interaction pool.** In every winning list that runs it
   (Rengar 3, Sett 3) it is part of a 7–8-card answer suite, not its own plan.
2. **The targets**, from the winners mapped onto these four jobs, bent for Sivir:

| winner | bodies | fight tricks | interaction | top end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rengar (1st, LA) | ~24 | ~5 | ~5 (+3 Faefolk pulls) | ~5 |
| Sett (3rd, Utrecht) | ~20 | ~9 | ~7 | ~5 |

   **Sivir's bend:** her legend is modest (about one Gold a turn, another after an enemy death),
   but the Gold is Power, so the jobs paid in Power (Punch First, Switcheroo, Rebuke: 2 Power
   each) are where this deck is cheaper than anyone's: the top of the range for tricks and
   interaction. Sivir, Mercenary is a guaranteed 4-drop that is 6 Might with Ganking after 2
   Power: half a top-end card, not a late threat.

| job | v1 today | **v2 target** |
|---|---|---|

| Fight tricks | 7 (Punch First 3, Switcheroo 2, Edge of Night 2) | **7** |

### The four jobs, named for Sivir's engine, with a spec each (28 Oct 2026)

*Jay: "we should be specific about each card… these are a bit generic vs the labels you gave
to other decks." The winners' jobs are named for how that deck does it ("bodies that pay off
when they die", "arriving where it hurts"); these are named for Gold and the trash. **The spec
is the test a card must pass to earn a slot in stage 4.***

| # | job | spec | v1 now | target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trash-and-recursion bodies**: take a battlefield, leave something behind | 3 Energy or less and 1 Power or less (cheap, and Matriarch can revive it); leaves value (a card, a Gold, a choice in the trash); survives pings (3 Might, or good with Edge) | Merchant 3, Ezreal 3, Fizz 3, Innovator 3, Matriarch 3, Tornado 2, Staunch 2 (19) | 17 |
| 2 | **Gold-paid fight tricks**: win the fight with Power, where Defy can't reach | 2 Energy or less; paid in Power; usable in the fight (Action, Reaction or from face down); out of Defy's reach (2+ Power, or it chooses your own unit) | Punch First 3, Switcheroo 2, Edge of Night 2 (7) | 7 |
| 3 | **Clear the battlefield you need**: break their hold, stop theirs (how the deck scores: hold one, take the other) | a **mix of kinds** (kill, bounce, stun, pull, strip) so there is an answer to every threat type; Power-paid preferred (Defy-proof); spells Fizz can recast (3 Energy or less) | kill: Rampage 3 · strip: Sabotage 2 · bounce: Star-Crossed 2, Rebuke 1 · lock: Vex 2 · pull: Evelynn 1 (11) | 10 |
| 4 | **Turn-5 threats that spend the spare Power**: outsize the board once Energy stops being tight | 5+ Energy or 6+ Might; out of Gust's range (4+ Might); **value when it lands**, since bounce is in nearly every top list; ideally a sink for spare Gold | Rengar, Trophy Hunter 2, Sivir, Mercenary (3) | 6 |

**What the specs show before stage 4:**
- **Job 4's spec is strict** ("value when it lands"): Rengar's Ambush is surprise, not value, so
  he meets it half way. The job to search for.
- **Staunch Survivor fails job 1's spec**: he leaves nothing behind (a sturdy body, not a value
  body). With bodies 19 → 17, he is the first card in question.
- **Job 3's mix is lopsided**: kill and bounce are 6 copies, lock 2, pull 1. Spreading the kinds
  points at more Rebuke or Vex, not more Rampage.
