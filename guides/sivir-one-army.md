## Executive summary: the game plan

*Read this before every round.*

**What the deck is.** A midrange deck that **holds one battlefield (call it A) and makes attacking it a mistake.** Cheap bodies hold A; a face-down card waits there; Ambush units and tricks punish whoever walks in. Then it turns the trap around: **Irresistible Faefolk pulls the other battlefield's defender into A, and Sivir walks into the battlefield they just left empty.**

**The closing turn, in one line.** Start your turn on **6 holding A**: the hold scores → **7**. Faefolk walks into A and pulls B's defender in; the fight happens at A (a face-down **Bedeviling Stranger** can bounce the unit you pulled); **Sivir** comes out of the Champion Zone with Accelerate and walks into the empty B → **8**. Holding at 7 also wins, on its own.

**Why it works.** Sivir is never in your hand to be discarded or drawn late, and the closing turn is cheap: Faefolk's walk and a face-down Stranger cost nothing, Sivir with Accelerate costs 5 Energy. Everything else you hold is spare for backup.

**What each part of the deck does.**

| job | cards |
|---|---|
| **Pull them in** | Faefolk ×3 · Evelynn (face down) |
| **Punish them at A** | Bedeviling Stranger ×2 · Existential Dread · Switcheroo · Edge of Night · Tideturner ×2 · Punch First ×3 · Nidalee ×3 and Rengar ×2 (Ambush) · Vex ×2 |
| **Hold A** | the two-drops (8 two-drop units, all Power-free) · Ember Monk (+2 whenever you flip a face-down card) · Boots of Swiftness and Edge of Night (+2 Might for good) |
| **Clear the way** | Rampage ×2 · Rebuke ×2 · Gust ×2 · Star-Crossed ×2 |
| **Close at B** | Sivir · Ride the Wind ×3 · Boots of Swiftness (Ganking: a holder walks A → B) |
| **Cards** | Ezreal ×2 · Innovator ×3 · Nidalee |

**Four rules for every game.**

1. **From 5 points, check every turn whether either player can reach 8 before the other acts again** ([strategy.md §0](../docs/strategy.md)). A hold at 7 wins and is never gated (§473.1.a.1).
2. **A is the whole game.** Spend cards to keep it; lose it and you lose the face-down card there with it (§323.6.a).
3. **Their Vex, Apathetic is gone before Sivir is played.** She stuns any unit you play while she's at a battlefield, wherever you play it, and a stunned Sivir can't walk to B.
4. **Hold Faefolk back.** It's a 1-Might unit whose job is one move on the closing turn. Keep one in base.

**What to bring:** game 1, **Sigil of the Storm** · after you lose a game (you'll choose to go first), **Amateur Recital** · after you win one (they choose, and usually go first), **Forbidding Waste** (§8).

- **30–31 October:** Jay's own rebuild around displacement (Faefolk, Ride the Wind); a three-reviewer audit; battlefields re-picked from all 71 legal ones.
- **31 October, the pros:** two experienced players said the deck can't win early fights; **play midrange, win on interaction on turns 3–4, carry top end that doesn't need Sivir alive, 8–9 cheap two-drops.** Nidalee ×3, Vex ×2, Gust ×2 in; Nilah out.
- **1 November, the trap:** −2 First Mate +2 Bedeviling Stranger, then −Portable Portal +The Good Stuff, then −2 Fizz −2 Staunch +2 Edge of Night +2 Tideturner (two experienced players: Fizz left the deck "with its foot in two camps", and Staunch wasn't a good two-drop), and last −1 Edge +1 Boots of Swiftness, −1 Switcheroo +1 Existential Dread, −The Good Stuff +Ember Monk (a reviewer doubted the deck holds well enough to feed The Good Stuff). Both cards had been cut on 31 October *for needing a held battlefield*; under the midrange plan that became the reason to play them. Punch First ×3, Ride the Wind ×3 and Rampage were questioned and kept on purpose; Staunch, Fizz, The Good Stuff, one Edge and one Switcheroo went in the later rounds (worksheet log).

## 3. The game, turn by turn

### Turns 1–2: claim A

- **A two-drop, walked into a battlefield.** Faefolk, Stranger, Tideturner and Evelynn all cost no Power, so turn 2 is never a rune short.
- **Which battlefield is A?** The one you can keep. Prefer the one they'd have to walk the furthest units into, and never Rockfall Path ("units can't be played here": it kills Ambush and Stranger). Forbidding Waste punishes a lone *defender*, so it's a better B than A.
- **Faefolk isn't your holder.** If it's your only two-drop, play it, but its real job is the closing turn. Keep at least one back in base from turn 4 on.

### Turns 3–4: arm the trap, win on interaction

- **Hide a card at A.** Stranger when their attacks will be 2-on-1 or wider; Switcheroo when fights will be 1-on-1 (Stranger needs 4 units, itself included). One face-down card per battlefield you control (§811.1.b), placed on your turn, playable from the **next turn on, theirs included**, at Reaction speed and for no cost.
- **Pay the hide with Gold when you can.** The cost is one Power of any colour: a Gold token pays it, or a recycled rune, and recycling a rune triggers the legend's Gold. A new Gold enters exhausted, so it pays from next turn on.
- **Keep Reaction answers up on their turn:** Gust (1 Energy) for their 2–3-Might attacker, Nidalee or Rengar by Ambush, Star-Crossed. This is where the pros said the game is won.
- **Put Vex at a battlefield.** Every unit they play is stunned, wherever it lands, so their Ambush blockers and ready-on-arrival units can't fight that turn.
- **Ember Monk and Boots on a quiet turn.** Monk at A defends at 6 whenever you flip a face-down card. Equip Boots (1 Chaos) on a holder at A: +2 Might, and Ganking lets it walk from A to B on the closing turn.

### From 5 points: the terminal scan

Every turn, both players: can either reach 8 before the other acts again? **Their win next turn outranks everything.** Then look for yours (§4). At 7 holding A, you have nothing to do but keep A.

## 4. The closing turn, rule by rule

Checked step by step against the rules on 1 November (handbook → verbatim → FAQ → rulings). **You start on 6, holding A, with Faefolk ready in base and ideally Stranger face down at A.**

1. **Beginning Phase:** you hold A → **7** (§315.2.b.2).
2. **Faefolk walks base → A.** You control A, so nothing is contested and no fight starts (§190.3.a.1). Its trigger moves **B's defender** to A. The pull is a target, so Baron Nashor can't be pulled (§355.7, §757).
3. **A fight starts at A at once.** Their pulled unit is the **attacker** (it arrived where they don't control), you're the defender (§466.2.c). There's no open window first: the cleanup stages a combat showdown and **they get focus first** (§323.12, §466.2.c.1.a).
4. **When focus reaches you, flip Stranger** (free from face down, §811.1.b; it goes to A, §811.1.d.1). Your holder + Faefolk + their unit + Stranger = 4, counted when the effect resolves (§383.4.a.2; the condition is checked on resolution, §383.2.a.1), so you may return their unit. **Don't pass while you hold focus**: if both players pass in a row, damage happens.
5. **No attacker, no damage.** You win the combat and keep A (§467.1, §468.5).
6. **B is empty.** It went uncontrolled in the cleanup after the pull (§323.5), and **any face-down card they had at B was trashed** (§323.6.a).
7. **Sivir:** play her from the Champion Zone with Accelerate: she enters ready at your base (§355.2.a, §805.6), 2 Power spent, 6 Might. **Walk base → B** (no Ganking needed, §144.4.a). You can only move once A's combat is over (§144.1.c).
8. **B's showdown** (you get focus first) closes with only your unit there: **conquer → 8** (§348.2.a.1). You scored A this turn by holding it, so the 8th point is open (§473.1.b.1).

**Stranger is insurance, not the line.** No Stranger? **Existential Dread** stuns the pulled unit (it's an attacker), so it deals no damage. Neither? The fight at A happens. If **any defender of yours survives**, their attacker goes to *their base*, not back to B (§468.1.a.2), so B is still empty and Sivir still walks in. Punch First or a face-down Switcheroo wins that fight. The only real danger is their pulled unit winning A: then they conquer A on your turn and score a point.

**At 6, holding nothing:** attack A with your board and move Faefolk in with the attack: its pull brings B's defender into A, and you stay the attacker there (§190.3.a.1, §466.2.c.1). Win the fight, conquer A → 7; then Sivir walks into the empty B → 8 (both battlefields scored this turn).

**At 5 holding A:** hold → 6, conquer the empty B → 7, and the next start-of-turn hold wins.

**Other second conquests** if Sivir is answered: Rengar by Ambush (even where only enemy units are), Ride the Wind (moves and readies, even in a showdown).

**What breaks it** (have an answer before you start):
- **Their Vex, Apathetic** at any battlefield: Sivir and any Faefolk played that turn are stunned and can't move. **Rebuke her first** (her Deflect makes it 3 Power, which turns on Sivir's condition by itself).
- **Responses to Stranger:** a Gust or Star-Crossed on Stranger makes the bounce miss (§811.1.d.3.a); killing one of your units drops the count to 3; Not So Fast counters the bounce *and* Faefolk's pull.
- **Answers to Sivir in B's showdown:** Rebuke, Hidden Blade, Moonfall, Star-Crossed, Siren's Song. Gust can't (she's 6).
- **Baron Nashor** can't be pulled or bounced, and **the Baron Pit** is a third battlefield: a conquest for the 8th point would need it scored too (this repo's reading, not a staff ruling). **Against Baron, win by holding at 7.**

**Sequencing against Defy** (counters a spell of 4 Energy or less and 1 Power or less, printed cost, §206): **safe:** Punch First, Rebuke, Switcheroo, every unit; **counterable:** Ride the Wind, Gust, Rampage, Star-Crossed, Existential Dread. Against Calm, close with units and the 2-Power spells.

**Ezreal's discount on Sivir:** take the **Energy** off her Accelerate, not the rune. A Power cost reduced to 0 isn't spent (§445.1.b), and she'd lose her +2 and Ganking.

## 5. The trap on their turn

They move into A. **Their units applied Contested, so they attack and get focus first** (§466.2.c.1.a). Your answers, in the order they're best:

| their attack | your answer | why |
|---|---|---|
| **2+ units, or 2-on-1 against your holders** | flip **Stranger**, return the biggest attacker | 4+ units counting Stranger; no Might limit; free |
| **1-on-1** | **Existential Dread** (stun it; Repeat to bounce), flip **Switcheroo**, or **Gust** a 3-Might attacker | Stranger only makes 3 units here and does nothing |
| **one big attacker** | **Existential Dread**, **Nidalee or Rengar by Ambush** into A, or flip **Edge of Night** (+2 Might for good) or **Tideturner** (swap a bigger unit in); **Ember Monk** at A gets +2 on any flip; Punch First if needed | Ambush plays them as a Reaction to a battlefield where you have units (§822) |
| **they flood A** | Graves from the sideboard | he deals his Might to every enemy there when he defends; no readying needed |

- **If no defender of yours survives, they conquer A**: your face-down card goes to the trash (§323.6.a). Equipment on the units there goes with them.
- **Kills ready your legend** (Battle Mistress: "When one or more enemy units die, ready me"), so she can make another Gold.
- **The trap works by doubt.** A face-down card at A could be Stranger, Switcheroo or Evelynn; the top Chaos decks all play the same package, so opponents know the threat but not the card. One reviewer called Stranger the best card in Radiance for exactly this ("how on earth is the opponent supposed to figure out how to attack?", Ultimate Guard, 20 October).

### The kill zone: the trap on your turn (Jay, 1 Nov)

You don't have to wait for them to attack A. **Bring their unit to it.**
1. **Ember Monk straight into A**: units can be played to a battlefield you control (§355.2.a).
2. **Faefolk walks into A and pulls one of their units in.** It arrives as the attacker; the fight starts at once and they have focus first (§466.2.c.1.a).
3. **Flip your face-down card in that fight.** Monk gets +2 (6 Might), and the flip does its own job: Edge +2, Tideturner swaps Rengar in, Switcheroo swaps Mights, Stranger bounces.
4. **Their unit dies.** The kill readies your legend for a Gold, and if it was B's only defender, B is empty for Sivir or a Booted holder.

**Best target: their Vex, Apathetic**, the card that breaks your closing turn. Pull her in and kill her the turn *before* you close (her Deflect makes the pull cost 1 more Power).
**Watch:** they act first in that fight (a Gust on Monk before he grows); their unit at A lets them Ambush more units in (§822.1.b), so count their open Energy; Monk's +2 is this turn only.

**The counter-plan is to ignore A.** Control decks hold B and build. That's what Faefolk and Evelynn are for: **the trap comes to them.** Pull their B unit into A on your turn, fight it where your tricks are, and Sivir takes B.

## 6. Card by card, with examples

Each card: what it says, its job in the trap, how to use it, and one worked example. The examples use real cards from the field; counts like "90%" are the share of that legend's legal lists since 23 October that run the card.

### The closer

**Sivir, Mercenary** (Champion · 4E 1P · 4 Might). *Accelerate. If you've spent at least 2 Power this turn, +2 Might and Ganking.*
- **Job:** walks into the empty battlefield for the last point. Never drawn, never discarded: she waits in the Champion Zone.
- **Use:** always with Accelerate on the closing turn (5 Energy, 2 Power: her own plus the Accelerate's Chaos), so she enters ready at 6 Might. Off the closing turn, she can also be a 4-Might holder at A. ("When one or more enemy units die, ready me" is your **legend's** text, not hers: a kill readies Battle Mistress for another Gold.)
- **Example (vs Kai'Sa, holding Recital, on 6):** hold → 7; Recital sends Kai'Sa's lone unit at B home. Sivir with Accelerate, walk into B. Kai'Sa answers with Smoke Screen (−4, so 2 Might) and then Hextech Ray (3 damage). Without help Sivir dies; with Unyielding Spirit cast in response to the Ray (it prevents all spell damage this turn) she survives at 2 Might, B's showdown closes, conquer → 8.

### Pull them in

**Irresistible Faefolk** ×3 (2E · 1 Might). *When I move to a battlefield, you may move an enemy unit to that battlefield.*
- **Job:** empties B on the closing turn by dragging its defender into A, where your tricks are.
- **Use:** any move triggers it, including an ordinary walk from base, so it can wait at base. A card's move counts too: a face-down **Tideturner** at A swapping with a Faefolk in base moves Faefolk to A, which is a pull at Reaction speed, on their turn as well; so does **Ride the Wind**. The pull chooses, so Baron and "can't be chosen" units are immune. Keep one in base from turn 4.
- **Example (vs Rengar):** you hold A with Tideturner and a face-down Stranger; Rengar holds B with one Pit Rookie. On your turn Faefolk walks into A and pulls the Rookie in. A fight starts at A, Rengar attacking with the Rookie, and he has focus first: he plays Thrill of the Hunt (2E 1P) to banish the Rookie and replay it at B. B had gone uncontrolled after the pull, so the replayed Rookie **conquers B and scores him a point on your turn** (§323.5, §348.2.a.1). **The lesson:** against Rengar (Thrill in 94% of lists), pull when he can't pay 2 Energy and 1 Power, or when his point doesn't matter.
- **Who attacks depends on who controls the battlefield.** Faefolk walking into an *uncontrolled* battlefield contests it, so **you attack** and the unit you pull defends (§190.3.a.1, §466.2.c.1; [Riftbound FAQ](https://www.riftboundfaq.com/cards/irresistible-faefolk)). Walking into A, which you control, makes the pulled unit the attacker. Playing Faefolk straight to a battlefield doesn't trigger it: that's a zone change, not a move (§448.2). Removing Faefolk in response doesn't stop the pull: the trigger already knows where it moved.
- **More ways to use it** (3 Nov 2026; Jun's notes, r/riftboundtcg, rules checked here):
  - **The early tempo play.** Played early, it gives them a bad choice: develop a unit you pull into a fight on your next turn, or skip their unit to dodge it. Walk into an empty battlefield, pull their two-drop, and Ambush Nidalee in beside Faefolk (you have a unit there). Jun calls it "the best 2-cost unit in the game"; players call it "a removal spell on delay". One early copy doesn't break "keep one in base from turn 4": you run three.
  - **Forbidding Waste (Jun).** Walk Faefolk **together with a real unit** into an uncontrolled Waste and pull one of theirs in: it defends alone at −2. Win the fight, conquer the Waste, and the kill readies your legend for a Gold.
  - **The hidden pull: face-down Tideturner at A, Faefolk in base.** Flip Tideturner and swap with Faefolk. Tideturner may choose a unit at another location (the errata; §811.1.d.3's own example), the swap is a move (§448.1), and only the *standard* move is barred mid-fight (§144.1.c, §451.1), so Faefolk's trigger fires: **a pull at Reaction speed they can't see coming.** Faefolk arrives ready (only a standard move exhausts, §144), and Ember Monk gets +2 for the flip. The pulled unit joins A's fight on its side (§466.2.c.3.a). On their turn it's an extra attacker at A, so use it when you win that fight; B starts your turn empty. It uses A's one face-down slot, so no Stranger that turn.
  - **Reuse.** Star-Crossed your own Faefolk back to hand (the rebuy), or let Recital's hold send it home from A to walk in again, at the cost of Recital's usual job.

**Evelynn, Entrancing** (Hidden · 2E · 2 Might · Backline). *When you play me from face down on your turn, you may move an enemy unit at a different location to my battlefield.*
- **Job:** the backup puller. Hidden at A, she's a pull you don't have to draw on the closing turn.
- **Use:** she can't share A's face-down slot with Stranger. Hide her when you have no Stranger, or at A while Stranger is still in hand. From face down **on your turn only**.
- **Example (vs Master Yi, Wuju Bladesman):** Yi holds B with one unit defending alone (+2 from his legend). Flip Evelynn at A on your turn: Yi's unit is pulled to A and attacks your two holders. It's no longer defending alone, so it loses the +2, and B is empty for Sivir.

### Punish them at A

**Bedeviling Stranger** ×2 (Hidden · 2E · 2 Might). *When you play me, choose a battlefield. If there are 4 or more units there, you may return an enemy unit there to its owner's hand.*
- **Job:** a free, Reaction-speed bounce with no size limit, at the battlefield you hold.
- **Use:** from face down it's played to A and counts itself (§383.4.a.2). It needs 4 units: two attackers into one holder works, a 1-on-1 doesn't.
- **Face down, it reaches only its own battlefield.** It is played to the battlefield where it was hidden (§811.1.d.1), and its play effect must choose its targets there (§811.1.d.3). The enemy unit it returns is a target (§355.12), and "choose a battlefield" doesn't rule out its own, so the Tideturner-style exception doesn't apply. A face-down Stranger at A can never bounce a unit at B. The trap doesn't need it to: the pulled unit is at A.
- **From hand it reaches any battlefield,** but as a plain 2-Energy unit: main phase only, no bounce mid-fight. Played to base it doesn't count toward the 4, so the chosen battlefield needs 4 units without it; played to a battlefield you control it counts itself. Against a wide board at B, bounce one of their four.
- **Example (vs Mordekaiser):** he attacks A with two units, one a 6-Might threat, into your Nidalee. Flip Stranger: Nidalee + Stranger + his two = 4. Return the big one to his hand. Nidalee (4 Might) handles a smaller one left behind, and bouncing rather than killing gives his deaths engine nothing.

**Switcheroo** (Hidden · 2E 2P · Action). *Swap the Mights of two units at the same battlefield this turn.*
- **Job:** wins the 1-on-1 fight Stranger can't touch.
- **Use:** hide it at A for 1-on-1 fights. From face down both targets must be at A (§811.1.d.2), and it costs nothing, so it spends no Power toward Sivir. From hand it's 2 Power, which turns Sivir on by itself.
- **Example (vs Riven):** Riven attacks A with a 7-Might Astral Heron into your lone Faefolk (1 Might). Flip Switcheroo: Heron is 1, Faefolk is 7 for the turn. Faefolk wins and the Heron dies (a 1-Might unit taking 7). She has Defy in 93% of lists, but Switcheroo's 2 Power puts it out of Defy's reach (§206).

**Punch First** ×3 (1E 2P · Action). *Give a unit +5 Might this turn.*
- **Job:** the cheapest way to win any fight, Defy-proof, and 2 Power that turns Sivir on.
- **Use:** in A's showdown on their attack, or on the closing turn if Stranger isn't there. Cast it after they've spent their Reaction, not before.
- **Example (vs Diana):** Diana attacks A and casts Smoke Screen (−4) on your Nidalee. You answer with Punch First (+5): Nidalee is 5 and wins. Diana's legend gives her an extra Energy she can spend only in showdowns, so she always has something for your fights: making her spend it on your terms is the whole matchup.

**Nidalee, Cat Form** ×3 (3E 1P · 4 Might · Ambush). *When I win a combat, draw 1.*
- **Job:** the Ambush defender. A 4-Might Reaction into A when they attack, and a card when she survives.
- **Use:** only "to a battlefield where you have units" (§822), so keep a holder at A. **Their Vex, Apathetic stuns her as she lands** (no combat damage that turn).
- **Example (vs LeBlanc):** LeBlanc attacks A with a 3-Might unit into your Faefolk. Ambush Nidalee into A: 4 + 1 against 3, she wins and draws.

**Rengar, Trophy Hunter** ×2 (5E 1P · 6 Might · Ambush). *Ambush. I can Ambush to a battlefield where there are enemy units, even if you don't have units there.* (errata; §822.1.d)
- **Job:** the big Ambush, and a second closer: he can land straight onto B.
- **Use:** on their turn into A; on yours onto B if Sivir is answered.
- **Example (vs Riven):** Riven holds B with two units. Faefolk pulls one into A; the other stays at B, and Sivir is answered. In your main phase, play Rengar straight to B (his Ambush reaches a battlefield with enemy units). Arriving there applies Contested (§190.3.a.1), so a fight starts at B with his 6 Might as the attacker.

**Vex, Apathetic** ×2 (4E · 4 Might · Deflect). *When an opponent plays a unit while I'm at a battlefield, Stun it. They can't move it this turn.*
- **Job:** shuts off their Ambush and ready-on-arrival units everywhere, while she's at any battlefield.
- **Use:** put her at A. Deflect taxes their answers to her by one Power.
- **Example (vs Rengar):** with Vex at A, Rengar's Ambush units arrive stunned and deal no combat damage that turn. His whole defensive plan (Ambush blockers when you attack) stops working.

**Rampage** ×2 (3E · Body optional). *Choose a friendly unit and an enemy unit. If you paid the Body, +2 Might to yours. They deal damage equal to their Mights to each other.*
- **Job:** the deck's only kill. Clears B's blocker before Sivir, or a holder of theirs.
- **Use:** main phase only. Clean against decks with no Calm (Rengar, Kennen, Evelynn); out against Defy decks and decks that want their units to die (LeBlanc, Mordekaiser). A kill readies your legend for another Gold.
- **Example (vs Evelynn):** her 3-Might unit holds B. Rampage with Nidalee (4, +2 with Body = 6): it dies, Nidalee survives, and B is empty.

### Clear the way

**Gust** ×2 (1E · Reaction). *Return a unit at a battlefield with 3 Might or less to its owner's hand.*
- **Job:** the cheapest answer: their 2–3-Might attacker, **or their Stranger before its bounce resolves** (it misses, §811.1.d.3.a).
- **Example (vs Riven, who runs Stranger in 85% of lists):** she flips her Stranger at her battlefield to bounce your attacker. Gust her Stranger (2 Might) in response: a face-down permanent's play effect reads its battlefield off the source, so with Stranger gone it misses (§811.1.d.3.a).

**Rebuke** ×2 (2E 2P · Action). *Return a unit at a battlefield to its owner's hand.*
- **Job:** any size, Defy-proof. **Your answer to their Vex, Apathetic.**
- **Example (vs Vex Gloomist):** on the closing turn, before you play Sivir, Rebuke her Vex. Her Deflect makes it 3 Power, which alone turns Sivir's +2 and Ganking on for the turn, and Sivir isn't stunned when she lands.

**Star-Crossed** ×2 (3E 1P · Reaction). *Return a friendly unit and an enemy unit to their owners' hands.*
- **Job:** a bounce at Reaction speed, or saving one of yours from removal at the cost of its place.
- **The rebuy (Jay, 2 Nov):** the friendly unit you return can be one you *want* back. Return Ezreal and replay him for another draw 2; return Innovator for another Accelerate trigger; return Faefolk for a second pull. Card draw and removal in one Reaction.
- **Example (vs Seraphine):** she casts Hidden Blade on your Sivir in B's showdown. Star-Crossed in response, returning Sivir to your hand and one of her units to hers: the Blade has no target, and Sivir is back in your hand to play again next turn. No conquest this turn, but no Sivir lost.

**Boots of Swiftness** (Gear · 3E · Chaos). *Equipment: +2 Might and Ganking. Equip: 1 Chaos.* (Ganking is printed in the bonus strip; our card data misses it.)
- **Job:** makes a holder bigger for good **and** gives it Ganking, so on the closing turn it can walk from A to the emptied B: a second closer that doesn't need Sivir.
- **Use:** equip on your turn to a holder at A. Keep a second unit at A when the booted one walks to B.
- **Example (vs Vex Gloomist):** her Vex, Apathetic stuns Sivir the turn you play her, so Sivir can't move. Your booted Nidalee was already at A: Recital sends B's defender home, Nidalee walks A → B (Ganking) and conquers. Vex only stuns units played that turn.

**Existential Dread** (1E 1P · Action · Chaos). *Stun an attacking enemy unit. If it's already stunned, return it to its owner's hand instead. Repeat: 2 Energy.*
- **Job:** the cheapest answer to an attack, up by turn 3, held in hand (no face-down slot). Stunned, it deals no combat damage; with Repeat it's bounced.
- **Use:** on their attack into A, or on **the unit Faefolk pulls into A on your closing turn** (it's an attacker there): stun it, and Stranger's 4-unit count doesn't matter. With Ezreal out, the Repeat costs 1 Energy. It has 1 Power, so Defy can counter it.
- **Example (vs Rengar):** he attacks A with a 6-Might unit into your Tideturner. Dread stuns it: no damage, A stays yours. Pay the Repeat and it's back in his hand.

### Close at B

**Ride the Wind** ×3 (2E 1P · Action). *Move a friendly unit and ready it.*
- **Job:** a second unit into B, or a holder out of trouble, from any battlefield to any battlefield, even mid-showdown (§451.1).
- **Example (vs Kennen, no Defy in 10%+ of his lists):** Kennen Star-Crosseds Sivir as she walks into B. In B's showdown, Ride the Wind your Nidalee from A to B (a card's move needs no Ganking, and it's readied too): the showdown closes with your unit there, conquer. **Check A first:** if Nidalee was your last unit there, A goes uncontrolled and your face-down card there is trashed (§323.5, §323.6.a).

### Hold A

**Tideturner** ×2 (Hidden · 2E · 2 Might · Chaos). *When you play me, you may choose a unit you control at another location. Move me to its location and it to my original location.* (errata)
- **Job:** a Power-free two-drop that works on their turn. From face down at A it swaps a bigger unit of yours into the fight for free; the errata lets it reach off its own battlefield (§811.1.d.3's Tideturner example).
- **Example (vs Kha'Zix):** he attacks A with a 4-Might unit into your Faefolk. Flip Tideturner at A and swap with Rengar at your base: Rengar (6) is now defending A, Tideturner sits at base. From hand it's a plain 2-Might holder.
- **The hidden pull:** swap with a **Faefolk** in base instead, and the swap moves Faefolk to A, so its pull fires (see Faefolk).

**Edge of Night** (Hidden gear · 3E · Chaos). *+2 Might equipment. When you play this from face down, attach it to a unit you control here. Equip: 1 Chaos.*
- **Job:** a face-down card that wins the fight at A **and stays**: +2 Might while attached (§137.3.a), not just for the turn like Switcheroo.
- **Use:** flip it in A's showdown onto the defender that needs it. From hand it's 3 Energy plus 1 Chaos to equip, so prefer it face down. It's gear: their Offer, Detonate and Acceptable Losses can kill it.
- **Example (vs Riven):** she attacks A with a 5-Might unit into your Nidalee (4). Flip Edge onto Nidalee: 6 against 5, Nidalee wins, draws a card, and keeps the +2 for the next attack.

### Cards and Gold

**Ember Monk** (4E · 4 Might · Chaos). *When you play a card from face down, give me +2 Might this turn.*
- **Job:** the holder that rewards the trap: 4 Might, 6 in any fight where you flip Stranger, Tideturner, Switcheroo, Edge or Evelynn.
- **Example (vs Kai'Sa):** Hextech Ray (3) doesn't kill him; with a flip in the same showdown he's 6.

**Reckless Innovator** ×3 (3E · 3 Might · Accelerate). *When I become exhausted, choose one: play a Gold gear token exhausted, or pay Chaos to draw 1.*
- **Job:** Gold (which pays your hides) or cards, every time it exhausts.
- **Example:** Accelerate it, walk it into A (it exhausts): take a Gold. Next turn that Gold, readied, pays to hide Stranger without recycling a rune.

**Ezreal, Prodigy** ×2 (3E 1P · 3 Might). *When you play me, discard 1, then draw 2. Optional additional costs you pay cost 1 Energy or 1 rune less.*
- **Job:** card flow on a body; discounts Accelerate and Rampage's Body.
- **Use:** on Sivir's Accelerate take the **Energy** off, not the rune: a Power cost reduced to 0 isn't spent (§445.1.b), and she'd lose her +2 and Ganking.

### The sideboard

- **Ruin Runner ×2** (6E · Body). *I can't be chosen by enemy spells and abilities.* The A holder they can't Gust, Moonfall, Rebuke, Star-Crossed, Charm or Blitzcrank away. It doesn't need to act; it needs to stay. **Example (vs Diana):** with Ruin Runner at A, her Moonfall and Gust have nothing there to aim at.
- **Graves ×2** (5E 1P · Body). *When I attack or defend, deal damage equal to my Might to all enemy units here.* Defending at A wipes a wide attack; defending needs no readying.
- **Acceptable Losses** (1E · Chaos · Action). *Each player kills one of their gear.* You pick yours: cast it with no gear out, or with only a Gold, or before you equip Boots or Edge. **Example (vs Orianna):** her only gear out is The Ball; it dies, and you lose nothing.
- **An Offer You Can't Refuse** (2E · Chaos · Action). *Kill a gear. Its controller plays a Gold.* You pick: Riven's Blade of the Exile (89% of her lists), The Ball, Gutter Palace.
- **Ravenbloom Prefect** (3E · Chaos). *When an opponent plays a gear, you may banish me to banish it.* **Against Ziggs, use this, not the two spells:** a Bomb that dies deals 2 to your unit; a banished one doesn't die.
- **Sabotage** (1E 1P · Body). *They reveal their hand; you recycle a non-unit card.* Takes Back Off, Switcheroo or Time Warp before it's hidden or cast.
- **Unyielding Spirit ×2** (1E 1P · Body · Reaction). *Prevent all spell and ability damage this turn.* Saves Sivir from Kai'Sa's Hextech Ray; blanks Bombs and pings.

## 7. Power, Energy and the odds

- **Energy is the bottleneck.** Hold up Gust and Ambush on their turn; equip Boots and play Ember Monk on turns you don't need to.
- **Power is cheap here.** The legend makes a Gold whenever you recycle a rune (if she's ready), and Gold pays Power of any colour, including the cost of hiding (riftbound.gg's Radiance write-up makes the same point about this legend).
- **A rune can do both in one turn:** exhaust for Energy, then recycle for Power (`docs/rules.md`, the rune formula).
- **Runes 6/6.** Power demand is Chaos 19, Body 13; turn-3 colour misses run 6–7% on the play and 4–5% on the draw at 6/6, and a 5/7 split measured within a point of that: under the 3-point bar for changing.

**How often you'll have a piece** (39-card library; about 11 cards seen by the closing turn):

| piece | copies | by 7 cards | by 11 cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| any two-drop unit | 8 | 83% | 95% |
| a puller (Faefolk or Evelynn) | 4 | 56% | 75% |
| Faefolk | 3 | 46% | 64% |
| Stranger | 2 | 33% | 49% |
| an answer to the pulled unit (Stranger, Existential Dread) | 3 | 46% | 64% |
| any trap card in hand (Stranger, Dread, Switcheroo, Edge, Tideturner ×2) | 8 | 83% | 95% |

Stranger alone is there about half the time, but with Existential Dread you have an answer to the pulled unit about two games in three, and some trap card nearly every game.

## 8. Battlefields, mulligans, sideboarding

**Battlefields.** In a best-of-3 each player picks one of their three, placed at once; one used in a decided game is retired (§488.5). Random at a single-game event (§487.5). **Battlefields are placed before turn order is chosen** (§488.5, then the start-of-game choice, §115): game 1 is blind, and in games 2–3 the loser of the previous game chooses the seat (TR §407.4) *after* seeing both (RiftJudge ruling: "The loser chooses turn order after battlefields are revealed"). So pick by the last result, not by the seat:

| when | battlefield | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Game 1 (blind)** | **Sigil of the Storm**: "When you conquer here, you must recycle one of your runes." | it doesn't reward holding, so it can't feed whoever sits on it. For you a recycle can make a Gold; for them a lost rune per conquest, which slows Baron and Astral Heron |
| **After you lose a game** (you choose, so go first) | **Amateur Recital**: "When you hold here, you may move a unit at a battlefield to its base." | **the trap's engine**, below |
| **After you win a game** (they choose, and going first wins more, so expect the draw) | **Forbidding Waste**: "While a unit here is defending alone, it has −2 Might." | no hold reward to hand them; it punishes their lone early holder. Hold it with two and the −2 never touches you |

**Rechecked after the Edge of Night / Tideturner swap (1 Nov):** all three stay. With 7 face-down cards for one slot at A (Stranger ×2, Tideturner ×2, Switcheroo, Edge, Evelynn), and Boots adding Ganking from A to B, the three still fit. **Bandle Tree** ("You may hide an additional card here") is the first battlefield to try if the slot keeps clogging, but its only sensible place is Recital's, and Recital is worth more.

**Amateur Recital, the engine.** Going first, your turn-2 move reaches a battlefield before theirs (unless they Accelerate a unit on turn 1: going second they have 3 runes). Make Recital your A. From your turn-3 hold, every turn:
- the hold scores, and its trigger (your Beginning Phase, before you draw) **sends their lone defender at B to its base** (§383.4.d.2.b);
- B goes uncontrolled once the trigger resolves and **any face-down card they had there is trashed** (§323.5, §323.6.a);
- in your main phase, walk a ready unit into B and conquer it. **Two points a turn.** From 6 it wins: hold → 7, conquer B → 8 (§473.1.b.1; Riot's FAQ gives this exact line).
- **Two defenders at B:** Recital sends one home, Faefolk pulls the other into the trap.
- **It chooses**, so Baron and "can't be chosen" units are immune, Deflect taxes it, and they can respond: a Reaction unit or a face-down card played at B keeps it occupied.
- **It's symmetric.** If they hold Recital, *their* hold sends your lone holder home and trashes your face-down card. Hold it with two, keep a face-down card there to flip in response, and **if they ever take it, retaking it comes first.**
- **Evidence:** none for it. 15 vouched lists since Radiance, no top-quarter finish; no guide recommends it; Ultimate Guard's go-first example is Targon's Peak, the safe alternative. It's here because a deck built to keep one battlefield is the deck its downside doesn't hurt. Log whether you claimed it and how often it fired.

**Mulligans** (set aside up to 2; they go to the bottom):
- **Want two two-drops and a 3-drop.** Keep Stranger, Faefolk, Tideturner, Evelynn, Switcheroo, Edge, Nidalee.
- **Send back first:** Rengar, Rampage, Star-Crossed and Boots in a hand with no two-drop.
- **Turn-1 two-drop:** 8 in the library, so 70% keeping your first 5 cards and 83% when you mulligan for one.

**Sideboarding** (untested; from the card reads in §9):

| in | against | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Ruin Runner ×2** | Vex Gloomist, Diana, Riven, Evelynn, Kennen, Mordekaiser, Akali, Irelia | a holder they can't Gust, Moonfall, Star-Crossed, Charm or bounce |
| **Graves ×2** | Rengar, LeBlanc, Seraphine, Master Yi | defends A against a flood: his Might to every enemy there when he defends |
| **Unyielding Spirit ×2** | Kai'Sa, Ziggs, Orianna, Akali | prevents all spell and ability damage this turn (Bombs, pings, Hextech Ray on Sivir, Frozen Fortress) |
| **An Offer You Can't Refuse** | Riven, Orianna, Jayce, Azir, Jarvan IV, Ornn | kills the gear you pick (they get a Gold) |
| **Acceptable Losses** | Riven, Orianna, Jayce, Azir, Jarvan IV, Ornn | each player kills one of their gear: cast it when yours would be a Gold or nothing |
| **Ravenbloom Prefect** | Ziggs, Orianna, Jayce, Azir, Jarvan IV, Ornn | banishes a gear as it is played (use it, not the spells, against Bombs) |
| **Sabotage** | Kai'Sa, Vex Gloomist, Seraphine, Kennen | takes Back Off, Switcheroo or Time Warp out of their hand before they hide or cast it |

What comes out depends on the matchup: §9's table has the exact ins and outs.

**Rampage comes out against Calm** (Riven, Vex, Akali, Irelia, Yi; K'Sante runs Defy too, but his answers are thin, so keep it unless it gets countered) and against decks whose units want to die (LeBlanc, Mordekaiser).

## 9. Matchups

### General rules, by archetype (3 Nov 2026, agreed with Jay)

**The default is slow: hold one battlefield, make them come to A.** Two exceptions race: **Baron decks** and **Ramp**, because their late game beats yours. Racing means going for the second battlefield sooner, still only when the terminal scan allows it. Archetypes and families are [archetypes.md](../docs/archetypes.md)'s map; the rules are drawn from the legend rows below and [strategy.md §2](../docs/strategy.md). **Not tested at a table.** The legend's own row overrides these.

| archetype | e.g. | their plan vs the trap | your pace | rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aggro** | Kha'Zix, Ambessa, Jarvan IV, Rek'Sai, Teemo | they walk into A early: **the trap gets fed** | **defend first, then hold** | mulligan hard for two-drops so A is armed by turn 3–4; Faefolk's early tempo play blunts their curve; Stranger and Dread on wide attacks; don't race them |
| **Tempo** | Diana, Irelia, Lillia, Ziggs, Evelynn | answers at Reaction speed to every piece | **patient, one hold** | **make them spend first**: bait with a lesser threat before Stranger or Sivir; close with Defy-proof cards (units, 2-Power spells); both battlefields only when it wins that turn (game 2) |

| **Control** | Kai'Sa, Vex, Seraphine, Akali, Jayce | won't attack A: **the trap idles** | **push: race to hold at 7** | Faefolk and Evelynn bring the trap to them; kill their Vex before Sivir; Stranger often comes out; Sabotage in |

### The local room in detail (Sip & Play)

**Rengar** (63 lists, 31 vouched; the widest deck here). **Best matchup.**
- He attacks, so A fills up; Stranger fires. He runs no Gust, Star-Crossed, Defy or Not So Fast in 10%+ of lists.
- **Watch:** Thrill of the Hunt (94%) saves a bounced unit or drops one into B against Sivir; **his own Faefolk (90%) pulls your holders out of A**, so keep two there; Brynhir (22%) stops you playing cards that turn.
- Your Vex at a battlefield stuns every Ambush unit he plays. Rampage is clean (no Calm). Graves ×2 in.

**LeBlanc** (57 / 20). **Good.**
- Wide and attacks; bounce is what she fears. **But** Deathgrip and Sacrifice (89% each) kill her own target in response: the bounce misses and she gets her death triggers. Bait them before the Stranger turn.
- Hidden Blade (84%) kills Sivir in B or a holder. Graves ×2 in against her wide attacks; Rampage out (deaths feed her).

**Kai'Sa** (93 / 37). **Good for the closing turn.**
- She doesn't walk into A; she kills your holders from range (Hextech Ray, Falling Star, Bellows Breath), and **Frozen Fortress** (89%) deals 1 to each unit there each Beginning Phase, killing a Faefolk.
- **No single card she can play in B's showdown on your turn kills a 6-Might Sivir, but two do:** Smoke Screen (Reaction, −4, in 97% of lists) takes her to 2, then Hextech Ray (3 damage, 100%) kills her. Close with **Unyielding Spirit** (prevents all spell damage that turn) in hand. She has no Gust, Star-Crossed or Vex in 10%+ of lists. Keep Faefolk in base; **never make Rockfall Path (90%) your A**; Brynhir (95%) turns the trap off for a turn.
- Unyielding Spirit ×2 and Sabotage in.

**Vex, Gloomist** (79 / 22). **Worst-two.**
- She holds and builds; she won't walk into A. **Her Vex, Apathetic (96%) stuns Sivir the turn you play her.** Back Off (91%), Defy, Discipline, her own Switcheroo and Stranger.
- **Plan:** Rebuke her Vex first (3 Power with Deflect, so it also turns Sivir on); or play Sivir a turn early. This is a race to hold at 7, not a trap game. Sabotage and Ruin Runner in; Rampage out.

**Diana** (41 / 12). **Worst.**
- Her legend's extra Energy works only in showdowns, so she always has an answer in your fights: **Gust (100%) on Stranger, Moonfall (100%) pulls Sivir out of B**, Vex (83%), Hard Bargain (76%) counters Switcheroo unless you pay 2 Energy, Star-Crossed, Eclipse, Smoke Screen.
- **Plan:** make her spend first; Gust her 2–3-Might bodies; Ruin Runner ×2 as holders she can't touch.

## 11. Mistakes that lose games

- **Playing Sivir into their Vex, Apathetic.** Stunned, she can't walk to B. Rebuke Vex first.
- **Passing with focus in the fight at A** before you've flipped Stranger.
- **Making Faefolk your only holder**, or playing every Faefolk early. Keep one in base.
- **Hiding Stranger for a 1-on-1.** It needs 4 units; hide Switcheroo there.
- **Losing A cheaply.** It takes your face-down card with it.
- **Choosing Rockfall Path as A.**
- **Trying to conquer for the 8th point with a Baron Pit out.** Hold at 7 instead.
- **Taking the rune off Sivir's Accelerate with Ezreal.** Take the Energy.
- **Equipping or casting Ember Monk on a turn you need Energy up** for their attack.

## 12. What to log

Each game, which of the four it was, so one problem isn't fixed with another's cards:
1. couldn't reach 6
2. reached 6 but couldn't open a battlefield
3. opened one but didn't make the second conquest
4. Sivir got answered

**Card draw first (the thing to watch):** did you ever have a turn with no useful card to play (an empty hand, or only cards that didn't fit)? On what turn? Did Innovator survive? Two or three games like that, with Innovator alive, means bringing back The Good Stuff (you're holding well enough to feed it) or a third Ezreal.

**And for the trap:** did it fire (a Stranger bounce, a pull into A, a hold at 7)? Was A ever lost, and to what? Which face-down card did you hide, and did it fit the fight? Review mistakes using [strategy.md §8](../docs/strategy.md)'s seven types.

**Open, for games to decide:** The Good Stuff back if A holds easily (it draws 3 every hold); Rengar #3 if games keep ending "Sivir got answered"; Bushwhack Trap (a second face-down card at A) if Evelynn and Stranger keep fighting for the slot.
