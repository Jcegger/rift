## 1. The Gameplan

This isn't a deck that races. It wins the game one battlefield disagreement at a time: drag
an opponent's key unit somewhere it doesn't want to be (Irresistible Faefolk, Evelynn,
Tideturner), then either strip the card that would have saved it (Sabotage) or bounce it
outright before it does anything (Gust, Star-Crossed). Every one of those movement triggers
also feeds card flow through Traveling Merchant's loot, and every rune you recycle turns
Battle Mistress into a slow-drip Power engine. Sivir herself is a single copy — a payoff for
a heavy Power turn, not the plan — and Baron Nashor is the two-copy insurance policy that
ends games once the disruption has worn the opponent down. It is built, deliberately, to be
a worse race and a better fight than almost everything ranked above it.

## 2. The Engine

Battle Mistress and Sivir aren't separate cards — they're one loop.

**Battle Mistress** (Legend, Body/Chaos) — *"When you recycle a rune, you may exhaust me to
play a Gold gear token exhausted. When one or more enemy units die, ready me."* Every rune
you recycle for Power is also a Gold token if you want it — and Gold's own ability is "kill
this, exhaust: Add one Power of any domain." That's a banked, on-demand rainbow Power you can
crack the instant you need to hit Sivir's threshold or splash a color you're short on. And
because she readies the moment *any* enemy unit dies — combat, Challenge, anything — she is
rarely tapped out for more than a turn once fighting starts.

**Sivir, Mercenary** (4 Energy / 4 Might, Chaos) — Accelerate. *"If you've spent at least 2
rainbow Power this turn, I have +2 Might and Ganking."* Only 2 Power (any domain, any mix)
unlocks a 6-Might unit that can hop battlefield to battlefield. Cast her after you know what
else you're doing that turn — Punch First or Switcheroo alone clears the bar on their own;
two 1-Power spells (say, Challenge + Sabotage) get there just as well.

## 3. Deckbook by Role

### Reposition & Disrupt Position (6 cards)

- **Gust** (1 Energy, reaction) — return a unit with 3 Might or less to hand. Your cheapest,
  most repeatable answer, and it hits every small token in the format.
- **Star-Crossed** (3 Energy / 1 Power, reaction) — bounce a friendly unit and an enemy unit,
  no Might cap. The answer when Gust is too small; spend a spare body of your own.
- **Flash** (2 Energy, reaction) — move up to 2 friendly units to base. Pure defense: pull
  units out of a losing fight or off a battlefield about to flip. Doesn't touch the opponent
  at all.
- **Tideturner** (2 Energy, Hidden, 2/2) — on play, swap places with a friendly unit. Walk a
  fresh body into a fight your opponent thought was safe.
- **Irresistible Faefolk** (2 Energy / 1 Might, Body) — whenever it moves, drags an enemy
  unit along with it. Turns your own Ganking and readying into forced regroups for them.
- **Evelynn, Entrancing** (2 Energy / 2 Might, Hidden, Backline) — played face-down, then
  drags an enemy unit to her battlefield on the reveal. The delayed, unseeable version of the
  same plan.

### Hand & Resource Disruption (2 cards, +2 sideboard)

- **Sabotage** (1 Energy / 1 Power) — reveal their hand, recycle any non-unit card. The
  single highest-value card against the whole Tier 1 (see the matchups below).
- **Hard Bargain** (2 Energy, reaction, Repeat 2 — sideboard) — counter a spell unless they
  pay 2 more Power. Your own answer to their Defy.

### Card Flow (3 cards)

- **Traveling Merchant** (2 Energy / 2 Might) — loots on every move. Pairs with all six
  repositioning cards above for free card selection.
- **Stacked Deck** (1 Energy) — look at 3, keep 1. Cheap smoothing, doesn't spend Power (so
  it doesn't feed Sivir).
- **Fizz, Trickster** (3 Energy / 3 Might / 1 Power) — replay a spell of 3 Energy or less
  from your trash for free (still pay its Power). A trap if cast turn one — sequence your
  cheap spells first, then Fizz to double one up.

### Combat Math (4 cards)

- **Punch First** (1 Energy / 2 Power) — +5 Might this turn. Cheap, huge, and — because it
  costs 2 Power — flatly immune to Defy.
- **Switcheroo** (2 Energy / 2 Power, Hidden) — swap the Might of two units at a battlefield.
  Also Defy-proof for the same reason.
- **Challenge** (2 Energy / 1 Power, Body) — your unit and theirs trade Might as damage. A
  precise two-for-one when your bodies out-Might theirs.
- **Here to Help** (2 Energy / 1 Power, Hidden, Body) — play a unit from hand to a
  battlefield you control for 3 less Energy. Best on Baron Nashor or Sivir.

### Threats & Finisher (4 cards)

- **Kha'Zix, Mutating Horror** (4 Energy / 4 Might, Ambush) — +2 Might and 2 XP against a
  lone enemy. Rewards the 1-on-1s the whole repositioning suite is built to force.
- **Rengar, Trophy Hunter** (5 Energy / 6 Might, Ambush) — can Ambush directly into an
  already-contested battlefield.
- **Vex, Apathetic** (4 Energy / 4 Might, Deflect) — stuns any unit the opponent plays at her
  battlefield, and it can't move that turn. The best card in the list against Ambush-heavy
  aggro.
- **Baron Nashor** (10 Energy / 12 Might / 3 Power) — can't be chosen by enemy spells or
  abilities, gives every other friendly unit +2 Might. The finisher the whole plan is built
  to survive toward.

### Battlefields (3 cards)

- **Emperor's Dais** — conquer: pay 1, bounce a friendly unit here, get a 2-Might Sand
  Soldier token here. Recurs an ETB for value.
- **Forbidding Waste** — a lone defender here gets -2 Might. Punishes anyone who leaves a
  single unit home alone.
- **Seat of Power** — conquer: draw 1 for each battlefield you or allies control. The best
  pure card advantage in the list once the board spreads out.

## 5. Matchups

### Kennen — Heart of the Tempest

*Order / Chaos · Tier 1, #1 · 9 decks sampled*

**Staples:** Lightning Rush (9/9), Ride the Wind (8/9), Stacked Deck (8/9), Traveling
Merchant (8/9), Nocturne, Horrifying (8/9), Rhasa the Sunderer (8/9), Minefield (8/9),
Star-Crossed (8/9)

Mills its own deck (Minefield, Kennen's own Burn 2, a discounted Rhasa the Sunderer that
costs 1 less per card in their trash) while replaying spells from the trash via Flow, then
pushes damage with cheap movement (Ride the Wind) and Heart of the Tempest's repeatable,
legend-fueled Assault 2 — free every time they play a card from anywhere but hand. You share
three cards almost verbatim with this list: Stacked Deck, Traveling Merchant, Star-Crossed.
This is fought on the same axis.

- **Their plan:** Bank Assault 2 onto one attacker per turn for free, snowball buffed swings,
  land a cheap huge Rhasa once the trash is full.
- **Your answer:** Sabotage a Lightning Rush or Ride the Wind before it fuels the loop. A
  reactive Gust/Star-Crossed on the Assault-buffed attacker, before combat damage, erases the
  whole swing for less than they spent building it. Vex punishes anything they replay from
  Hidden or mill into play at her battlefield.
- **Watch for:** Rhasa can be absurdly cheap by turn 5–6 once their own trash is full — a
  10 Energy / 6 Might body that might cost 2–3. Forbidding Waste punishes it the moment it
  has to defend alone.

### Master Yi — Wuju Bladesman

*Calm / Body · Tier 1, #2 · 15 decks sampled*

**Staples:** Charm (14/15), Defy (14/15), Discipline (13/15), First Mate (13/15), Rengar,
Trophy Hunter (13/15), En Garde (12/15), Sabotage (12/15), Punch First (12/15), Master Yi,
Tempered (12/15)

Not the same Yi as your own Rengar, Trophy Hunter tech — but this exact card sits in 13 of 15
recent lists, right alongside Sabotage and Punch First, both of which you also run. A
trick-dense combat-math midrange: Charm drags your units to bad battlefields,
Discipline/En Garde/Punch First win combat steps outright, Defy protects their key plays from
your removal, and Master Yi, Tempered slowly banks XP via Hunt (conquering/holding) toward
Level 6 Deflect + Ganking.

- **Their plan:** Win individual combat steps with cheap tricks, protect the ones that matter
  with Defy, let Yi level up into a mobile, hard-to-remove body.
- **Your answer:** This is the single best matchup for Sabotage — strip Defy or Discipline
  out of hand before combat, and your removal goes through clean. Punch First and Switcheroo
  both cost 2 Power, over Defy's 1-rainbow cap — they're flatly Defy-proof already (see
  section 6).
- **Watch for:** Level 6 is gated on the player's XP total, a permanent resource — bouncing
  Yi doesn't undo it. Answer him before 6 XP is banked, not after.

### Irelia — Blade Dancer

*Calm / Chaos · Tier 1, #3 · 13 decks sampled*

**Staples:** Stellacorn Herder (13/13), Boots of Swiftness (13/13), Defy (11/13), Discipline
(11/13), Ride the Wind (11/13), Defiant Dance (11/13), Scuttle Crab (10/13)

A movement-value engine like yours, but built to snowball instead of disrupt. Blade Dancer's
legend readies a chosen unit for rainbow Power, or readies itself for 1 on conquer — one unit
can conquer and attack repeatedly in a turn. Stellacorn Herder draws on every move, Boots of
Swiftness hands Ganking to anything, Targon's Peak refunds 2 runes on conquer to keep the
loop running.

- **Their plan:** Ready and re-ready the same handful of units to conquer, attack, and draw
  multiple times a turn.
- **Your answer:** Same Defy/Discipline overlap as the Yi matchup — Sabotage and your
  Power-heavy tricks apply the same way. But the real pressure point is tempo: Stellacorn
  Herder is only 3 Might, a clean Gust target, and denying it directly breaks their loop
  rather than just trading resources. Irresistible Faefolk dragging a readied attacker
  somewhere they didn't plan for wrecks their sequencing outright.
- **Watch for:** Boots of Swiftness grants Ganking to literally anything — never assume a
  unit is stuck at one battlefield just because it isn't naturally mobile.

### Azir — Emperor of the Sands

*Calm / Order · Tier 1, #4 · 11 decks sampled*

**Staples:** Azir, Sovereign (11/11), Hidden Blade (11/11), Brutalizer (11/11), Guards!
(11/11), B.F. Sword (11/11), Deathgrip (11/11), Arise! (11/11)

The one Tier-1 deck sharing no domain with you at all. A go-wide Equipment/token deck:
2-Might Sand Soldier tokens flood in via Guards! and the legend's own activated ability
(scaled by Equipment count), all with free Weaponmaster; B.F. Sword/Brutalizer/Doran's
Shield/Eye of the Herald buff whoever's wearing them; Hidden Blade and Deathgrip are the
removal.

- **Their plan:** Flood 2-Might tokens, buff the board with Equipment, remove your answers
  with Hidden Blade/Deathgrip.
- **Your answer:** Bounce is secretly much better here than it looks: Sand Soldiers are
  *tokens*, and a token sent to hand doesn't go to hand at all — it simply ceases to exist.
  Every Gust on a 2-Might token (well inside its 3-Might cap) is a permanent kill, and
  sideboard Downwell wipes their whole token board for good in one shot. Forbidding Waste
  quietly punishes every lone token forced to defend alone.
- **Watch for:** Equipment survives its wearer dying and moves to something else — killing
  one body doesn't remove the gear from the game. Sideboard Akshan, Mischievous (pay 2 Body
  extra) steals it outright instead. And Downwell bounces your own board too — Baron Nashor's
  "can't be chosen" doesn't protect him from an unconditional effect — so only fire it when
  you're light on units yourself.

### Rengar — Pridestalker

*Fury / Body · Tier 2, #1 · 4 decks sampled (small sample)*

**Staples:** Noxus Hopeful (4/4), Darius, Trifarian (4/4), Sabotage (4/4), Punch First (4/4),
Inferna (4/4), Irresistible Faefolk (4/4), Nidalee, Cat Form (4/4)

A different Rengar build from the Rengar, Trophy Hunter unit sitting in your own list — this
is the aggro legend, ranked Tier 2 #1, though only 4 lists surfaced in the last 60 days.
Ambush units (Inferna, Grim Apothecary, Nidalee) jump straight into a fight already in
progress; Noxus Hopeful and Legion discounts keep the curve low; Darius and First Mate chain
extra buffed attacks. You share Sabotage, Punch First, and even Irresistible Faefolk with
this exact archetype.

- **Their plan:** Ambush into fights you thought were settled, chain cheap discounted bodies
  into extra buffed attacks, race before you stabilize.
- **Your answer:** Vex, Apathetic is the best card in the 56 against this deck: she stuns
  anything played at her battlefield — Ambush included, which normally dodges this kind of
  interaction entirely — and locks it from moving. Prioritize board control over grinding;
  Gust and Challenge trade favorably into their 1–2 Might Ambush bodies.
- **Watch for:** only 4 recorded lists in 60 days — treat this section as directional, not
  settled.

## 6. Beating Defy

Defy is the single most-played piece of interaction across the top of the format (14/15 Yi
lists, 11/13 Irelia lists) — it counters "a spell that costs no more than 4 Energy and no
more than 1 rainbow Power." Check your own suite against that line:

| Vulnerable | Cost | Defy-proof | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gust | 1 Energy / 0 Power | **Punch First** | 1 Energy / 2 Power |
| Flash | 2 Energy / 0 Power | **Switcheroo** | 2 Energy / 2 Power |
| Star-Crossed | 3 Energy / 1 Power | | |
| Challenge, Sabotage, Here to Help | ≤2 Energy / 1 Power | | |

Punch First and Switcheroo both cost 2 Power — over Defy's 1-rainbow cap — so they simply
cannot be countered by it, full stop. And Hard Bargain from the sideboard is itself a
spell-tax reaction: hold it up against a Defy you're expecting, and you counter their
counter. Practical sequencing: lead with a cheap, expendable spell to bait Defy out, then
land the real play once you know it's spent — or once you know it was never in danger to
begin with.

## 7. Sequencing & Mulligans

- **Mulligan for** one repositioning piece, one card-flow piece, and a body that survives to
  turn 3–4. A hand with Baron Nashor and nothing else is not a plan — it's a 10-Energy payoff
  with no path there.
- **Feed Sivir on purpose.** She only flips on with 2+ Power spent that turn — Punch First or
  Switcheroo alone clears it; two 1-Power spells work just as well. Decide your whole turn
  first, then cast her.
- **Run the Battle Mistress loop.** Recycle a rune, exhaust her for a Gold token, and hold
  the token until you need exactly one more rainbow Power — for Sivir's threshold or an
  off-color splash. Every kill readies her back up.
- **Don't Fizz too early.** It's only good once your trash has something worth rebuying.
  Spend your cheap spells first, then Fizz to double one up.
- **Hold Flash and a spare Gust** on turns where you expect a big combat swing or a board
  wipe, rather than tapping out.

## 8. Sideboard Guide

8 of 10 slots are already locked in. What actually moves between games:

| Vs. | Bring in | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kennen | Hard Bargain x2 | Their whole shell leans on cheap Flow/reaction pieces — tax them. |
| Master Yi | Hard Bargain x2, Ruin Runner | Defy is the card worth blowing out; Ruin Runner dodges Charm-into-combat lines. |
| Irelia | Hard Bargain x2, spare Gust | Same Defy problem as Yi; small units are everywhere in this list. |
| Azir | Akshan, Ruin Runner, Downwell | Steal their Equipment outright; dodge Hidden Blade/Deathgrip; wipe the token board for good. |
| Rengar | spare Gust, (consider Ruin Runner) | Prioritize cheap trades and Vex's stun over grindy card flow — this is a race. |

Cuttable when boarding in: Flash (does nothing to the opponent), one Here to Help, or one
Stacked Deck — the slowest, most situational cards in the 56. Treat this table as a starting
point, not a fixed list; true it up against your own games.
