## 1. What the post-ban format asks of you

In his read of the CCS $25k Houston results (21 September), the format sets three tests, and a deck that fails any of them accepts an unwinnable matchup:

1. **Be fast, or survive an early Dazzling Aurora.** Jayce, Aurora went from B-tier to arguably the best deck overnight. He calls the matchup variance-heavy: did they find Aurora on time, and did you find the answer on time?
2. **Be "semi good at holding" against the Heron decks** (Irelia and Akali). If you let a Heron player conquer for free and drop Astral Heron without being offered a fight, you fall very far behind.
3. **Have a plan against the big combat decks**, Rengar and Azir.

**This is why the list became hold-centric.** He kept midrange as his build because it answers all three. **Kinkou Initiate** replaces Stacked Deck's card selection with card draw, on a body, and it is easy to burn and replay with Tail-Cloaked Matriarch. The **Hidden cards** fix the cost of drawing more than you can play: a hidden card costs one Power now instead of its Energy later, so your extra cards become **midgame holds that Heron decks cannot break cheaply**.

The other two builds he tested are shelved. **Aggro** develops too little and cannot offer good holds against Heron, and without Stacked Deck it runs out of cards. **Late-game** (Illaoi into Baron Nashor) bent his play around saving runes and too often failed to find Nashor. He will retest it in Radiance.

**His matchup read, post-ban:** Jayce and LeBlanc have "always been superb" for Kha'Zix. Irelia and Akali are decent. Rengar and Master Yi are even or slightly unfavoured, "nothing we can't beat". Kennen is much weaker since the ban.

## 2. The game plan in one paragraph

You are the proactive deck and they are the reactive one. They can never safely pass with no runes open, because you are always threatening removal and a conquest. **Develop units first, and use spells only once the board can make them count.** The spells are the deck's selling point, but they are weak without units behind them. **The target is 6 points before your opponent.** Your finishers (above all Void Assault) can force units out of holds, so you need very little setup for the double conquest that takes the 7th and 8th points. That end-game is why you can invest heavily to reach 6, **as long as you still hold the cards for the last turn.** Do not spend everything on the 6th point and arrive out of gas.

---

## 3. Patience, development and the curve

- **Patience is the deck's first skill, and impatience is punished.** Units first, then spells. Units cost less Power than spells, so developing first also builds your rune economy.
- **Two standard openings.**
  - **Tempo start:** Irresistible Faefolk on turn 1, then Kha'Zix on turn 2. You trade your turn-1 play for theirs. If they skip their turn-1 play to dodge the Faefolk, switch to the developmental start.
  - **Developmental start:** a 2-drop on turn 1, a 4-drop on turn 2. You are happy to be slower, because any free point they take gets punished by a conquest that kills their unit.
- **Turn 3 is flexible, and he usually develops again.** A developed Zed can win the game.
- **Power is expensive early.** His illustration: in a seven-turn game, a Power spent on turn 2 costs about five Energy over the game. That is why Punch First and the Power spells wait for the midgame. It is also why **Sandswept Tomb matters**: it saves Power on every friendly-targeting spell cast there.

---

## 4. Points, holds and playing from behind

- **Score with expendable bodies.** Up from the Deep's Tentacles score at open battlefields. If the opponent kills them to conquer, your developed base kills their units in turn, scores again, and the XP from the win walks your unit home. Losing Tentacles to that exchange is a good trade.
- **Behind on points is fine if you are ahead on development.** Let them score first, then enter, win the fight and retreat. Some games you develop for several turns and then wipe their board in one turn with Void Assault, Faefolk and Rampage.
- **"The best defence is a good offence."** The player in the lead who can mostly conquer is better placed than the player holding to keep up. Kha'Zix may be the best deck in the format at this, because the legend retreat leaves nothing behind for the opponent to hit.
- **But you must hold sometimes, and that is where the post-ban list differs.** He calls holding "the single most powerful mechanic to gain points" and also "the single most risky", because a bad hold often loses the game. A good Kha'Zix player avoids bad holds. **A great one finds the holds that are required.** His test: **can I win this game without a hold?** If the answer is no, force one, midgame or on the last turn. It may fail, but refusing to hold in a losing game guarantees the loss.
- **At 7, hold with a split board plus a hidden Tideturner.** Dividing your units across both battlefields with a facedown Tideturner behind them "can really mess with your opponent's attacks."

## 5. How Jun uses the cards you actually run

Only the cards in your current 56, in his words where it matters.

- **The legend.** Its best use is **walking a unit home after it conquers**, so the inevitable retake kills nothing. The buff is for **moving a unit past removal and Might thresholds**: 7 Might is much safer than 6 against Singularity or Falling Star.
- **Kha'Zix, Mutating Horror is a combat trick that happens to develop your base, not a unit to develop.** The standard play: Ambush onto an isolated enemy unit and attack as 6 Might. That pays 3 XP, 2 from the trigger and 1 from the win, and you spend 2 of it walking him home. **He seldom plays Kha'Zix to base and passes.** That is also why Kha'Zix does not count as your turn-2 play when you mulligan.
- **Irresistible Faefolk** is the best 2-cost unit in the game. On turn 1 it either stops their turn-1 unit or gets it killed next turn. It stays live as removal all game and helps finish games late.
- **Shadow Order Disciple** is the second-best 2-drop: it burns cards and grows when it moves. On the draw it slows the game down, because their 2-drop does not want to score a point and then die to it.
- **Traveling Merchant** handles filtering, filling the trash, and drawing even when you have nothing to discard. It is your plan for when you run low on cards.
- **Tideturner** gives you movement onto the opposing battlefield, a pseudo-Ganking. Mostly, though, it is **another hidden card for holds.**
- **Fizz, Trickster** is a 3-Might body plus a free spell from the trash, for 3 Energy and 1 Power. Keep him for the late game, where he takes games over.
- **Vex, Apathetic** is a strong turn-2 play: 4 Energy, no Power, and Deflect makes her hard to remove. **Plan B:** once their units are cleared, leave Vex on a battlefield. The threat of scoring to 6 on both battlefields while she stuns anything they play is a real way to win.
- **Tail-Cloaked Matriarch** is a turn-2 developer whose Empower is still a threat several turns later. In the late game, **keep her in hand and reveal her on the turn you Empower immediately**, bringing back Fizz above all. That is a lot of pressure out of nowhere.
- **Rengar, Trophy Hunter** is a 6-Might unit that jumps in with no setup, and he can often hold afterwards. **Rarely play him on their turn**, even though you can. You want to be sure you win the fight, and that is easiest on your own turn.
- **Zed, Without a Sound** pulls an *even* game your way, where Rengar gets you back in from behind. He is slow and fragile at 5 Might. Play him on curve only if they cannot clear him from your base. Otherwise Sabotage first, or combine him with Void Assault in one turn. **Getting even one Shadow Clone is enough**, so do not get greedy.
- **Sabotage.** Save it for turns where a lot of combat will happen, or use it *defensively*: before you play a unit you cannot afford to lose, check the coast is clear.
- **Punch First** is the strongest Might swing in the game for 1 Energy. Its steep Power cost makes it a midgame-to-late card, and Defy cannot counter it. **Do not force early fights with it.**
- **Void Assault** is the best card in the deck, and he argues it is the best card in the game. It scores and removes a unit in one card, and it is the final-two-points card. **Do not use it like a Charm.** If another card can remove that backline unit (Faefolk, Star-Crossed or Rampage), save Void Assault. The test: *does my own unit need to move this turn?*
- **Rampage** is the deck's way to kill a unit without moving it, which matters against units that punish being moved. Save it for the key unit (their champion, or Stellacorn Herder against Irelia), or fire it in the last turns, when bodies matter most.
- **Up from the Deep** gives two bodies for scoring and good trades, and it stays useful from the trash. The ideal sequence: turn 1 on the draw, score twice on turn 2, then develop, for example with Zed.
- **Star-Crossed** punishes heavy development or an expensive conquest. Bounce your own Fizz to replay him. **Keep it for emergencies**: a lost fight, or a big hold you need to break.
- **Switcheroo.** Its best use is not a flat swap. **Attack barely over their hold with a unit plus a Tentacle. Make them spend a pump to save the fight, then swap the Tentacle's Might with their pumped unit.** That is how the card earns its cost. It is better than it looks against big-Might decks for the same reason.
- **Gust** (side). It matters less now, but it is still **the main answer to Kennen replaying Ride the Wind**: wait for the Ride the Wind and react. One or two copies.
- **Hard Bargain** (side). Almost never dead. Decks hold up *just* enough for one pump and spend the rest developing, so a counter wins the fight and kills the unit. **He holds it for his own turn**, and only holds Energy for it on their turn against expensive spells (Singularity, Arise!) or when he has nothing else to spend Energy on.
- **Acceptable Losses** (side). Cheap enough to fit any curve. It answers a hidden Zhonya's Hourglass for 1 Energy, and Fizz replays it, so they must respect it whenever it sits in your trash.
- **Decree of Strength** (side). Sabotage is still better, but this one costs no Power and covers several hard matchups. One copy is cheap insurance.
- **Ravenbloom Prefect** (side). Mainly for Dazzling Aurora, where it is "incredibly oppressive": if they cannot remove it, they cannot play the game, and Matriarch revives it. He would side it in often on the draw, to play on turn 1.

**Cards he deliberately does not run,** in case you are tempted:
- Grim Resolve: early pumps cost development.
- Spirit Wheel: too slow.
- Gust Monk, Noxian Demolitionist and Nidalee: Nidalee is better in Rengar.
- First Mate and Lucian: Up from the Deep does Lucian's job longer.
- Kennen, Storm of Shuriken: Fizz already does it.

He also ran **Kinkou Initiate** only reluctantly before the ban, because it draws less reliably in Kha'Zix than in Rengar. **Ride the Wind** he called a mediocre Void Assault in a deck that taps out on its own turn. Both are in the post-ban list anyway, which tells you how much the ban changed.

### Interactions worth knowing

- **Fizz + Void Assault:** develop a body, start a fight and score from an empty board. Players feel safe when you have no units.
- **Fizz + Rampage:** 3 Energy plus 2 Power for 5 damage anywhere and a body.
- **Fizz + Up from the Deep:** mass early development. The earlier the better, but early Power is costly.
- **Fizz + Star-Crossed:** bounce Fizz and replay him, taking another spell from the trash.
- **Faefolk + Tideturner:** flip Tideturner on their turn and swap with Faefolk, which drags an enemy unit to that battlefield. Use it when your hold is safe and you want their other conquest to be harder.
- **Faefolk or Void Assault + Forbidding Waste:** drag a unit there so it defends alone at −2. Forbidding Waste is no longer in your 56, so this one is for when you build differently.
- **Zed + Void Assault:** attack with the Shadow Clone to get Assault 4, swap it with Zed, then Void Assault the Clone back in for a second Assault 4. Effectively 8 Might, for breaking a big hold.
- **Zed + Switcheroo:** Zed is pumped into their hold and they answer with their own Punch First. Swap Zed out for a Clone, Switcheroo the Clone with their biggest unit, swap Zed back in, conquer, and lose nothing.
- **Vex holding + Rengar in hand:** pass and play reactively. If they conquer the other battlefield, Ambush Rengar there. If they only develop, Vex stuns their units and Rengar comes down next to her.
- **Star Spring + a hidden unit (post-ban).** Conquer Star Spring with a strong unit such as Vex or Rengar, then hide a card there. If the hold looks bad on their turn, flip the hidden unit. Star Spring then moves your strong unit home: in his words, a "1-Power, 0-Energy Flash". It works under the rules. Playing from Hidden is playing the card (§811.1.b), a hidden permanent must be played to that battlefield (§811.1.d.1), and a unit moved to base "is no longer in combat" (§359.3.e.5's own example). One caveat: Star Spring fires only on **the first time** a non-token unit is played there each turn, so check that nothing has used it before you flip.

## 6. Battlefields and mulligans

**Battlefields.**
- **Zaun Warrens is his game-1 default**, "besides some exceptions". You choose what to discard, so you fuel the trash synergies on your own terms, and you can run narrow cards and pitch them in the matchups where they are dead. That makes a symmetric battlefield asymmetric in your favour.
- **Sandswept Tomb is usually saved for going second in games 2 and 3.** Its job is saving Power on your friendly-targeting spells, and your list has about ten of them. He would save up to 4 Power a game.
- **Star Spring is the battlefield he hates and runs anyway.** Its best case is a turn-1 Faefolk, which he gives you only 45.7% of the time, and Kha'Zix has one reliable Ambush unit against Rengar's ten-plus ways to trigger it at Reaction speed. **So it is risky against Rengar.** The hidden units are what earned it the slot ([§5](#interactions-worth-knowing)).

**Mulligans.**
- **Always have a unit for turn 1 and turn 2. If you do not, put two cards back.** Kha'Zix does not count as your turn-2 unit, for the reason in §5.
- **Removal is interchangeable.** What matters is having some, not which, so with several removal spells in hand, recycle some.
- **Mulliganing a 1-of loses it for the match**, so sometimes keep the Rampage over the second Void Assault. Two different cards beat two copies of one.
- **When in doubt, keep Void Assault.** Several copies of it in a game is never a losing plan.

## 7. Sideboarding

**The principles, which still hold:**

- **The curve comes first.** On the draw, trim 2-drops. On the play, trim some 3s and 5s. Don't do either blindly: ask whether a 3-drop is a good turn-2 play *in this matchup*, or whether your 4s are simply better.
- **Keep the unit-to-spell balance.** Most side cards are spells, and units are the easiest cuts. Taking out six units for six spells is rarely right, so cut the least useful *spells* first.
- **Variety beats copies.** Two different cards usually do more together than two of one.

**His tables are for the pre-ban sideboard**, and most of their cards are not in your 10. What still transfers is which of *your* cards he brings in or takes out:

## 8. Matchups

His six write-ups, written against the pre-ban list, with his post-ban read in [§1](#1-what-the-post-ban-format-asks-of-you).

- **Kennen: keep the pressure high and deny their setup.** Kill Kennen, Storm of Shuriken the moment it lands. Kennen cannot both contest your board and build its trash, so make it choose. Sabotage away Last Rites, ideally the turn before they would play it. Expect a 10+ Might mid-game hold (a conquest followed by Rhasa, the Sunderer), and expect their setup to be complete by turns 4–5. So score early with expendable bodies, and keep Punch First and a developed base to break that hold. If they get set up anyway, a one-turn board wipe with all your removal cuts off their Last Rites recursion. Get to 6 first.
- **Rengar: be patient, and do not bleed points.** Both decks develop and tap out, so remove their units with Void Assault while scoring. Their early game is stronger, and one misstep there can lose the game. **Punch First is both players' trump card.** Enter combat only when you are already winning it, so they have to act first: they spend their Punch First, then yours wins.
- **Master Yi: develop hard, then hold.** Yi has the stronger early and mid game, and your late-game spells force bad fights for them. **This is one of the few matchups where you actively want to hold.** Make them invest heavily each time they retake a battlefield, because resources are scarce for Yi. Use your movement to strip the solo-defender +2: move their units so they attack into your hold, or make them hold with more than one unit.
- **Azir: let them have the early game.** Their units are cheap and leave Equipment behind, and Azir usually develops only one unit a turn and runs out of cards. So develop, accept their early point lead, and claw back with removal: a board they cannot rebuild if you clear it all at once. Watch your rune economy, know which hidden cards to play around, **use Sabotage to take their non-unit cards**, and play around Arise!
- **Irelia: favoured for Kha'Zix, so press.** Score a lot early without losing value, and once you reach 6 first they need an extraordinary hold that their low-unit plan rarely builds. Punish a greedy tap-out with removal. **Be patient with your spells and aggressive with your units.** Giving up units for points is fine if you are not making unnecessary sacrifices.
- **Diana: read their open Energy.** Each turn, ask whether you win the fight against what they have left open. If yes, punish them for spending runes on development. If they kept too much up, develop instead. They hold well and get stronger very late, so do not stall too long. They split their Energy between developing and interacting, so pressure the side they skimp on. Use Sabotage when they hold Energy up, but only once your board is developed.

## 10. Jun on video

Three of Jun's YouTube videos, transcribed from their auto-generated captions and summarised in our words. The Metafy-only matchup posts (Akali, Jayce and LeBlanc, 22–23 September) are not on YouTube.

### "Top 5 mistakes Riftbound players make" (29 November 2025)

His first video, and general rather than Kha'Zix-specific. It is consistent with [strategy.md](../docs/strategy.md) point for point.

1. **Actions and Reactions.** Reactions are the stronger tool, because they answer anything that opens a chain, so spend them sparingly. **Casting a spell pre-emptively, before the fight, can win you information.** His example: removal on their unit before you attack. If they answer it with a pump, you change your attacks. If they don't, you attack and keep your own trick for their response.
2. **Sequencing.** Before any move or play, think the whole turn through: am I forcing a showdown, am I playing a unit, am I holding runes for Actions or Reactions? **Moving and conquering before you play units keeps more options open, and shows the opponent less** before they have to decide on interaction.
3. **Mulligan for the matchup.** You know who goes first and what they play, so picture how the game goes, not just your curve. A curve-perfect hand of 2-drops can be wrong against a deck whose early units are bigger than yours.
4. **Role recognition.** You are the aggressor or the defender, and that can change mid-match. The aggressor forces trades and scores before the opponent stabilises. The defender preserves units and avoids inefficient early trades.
5. **Holding.** Their points do not slow your own clock, so **letting them score is fine if it improves your position.** **The defender has already revealed what is defending**, and the attacker builds the fight around that, which is the first-in trap from strategy.md in his words. So **retreating is often right even from a winning position**: "retreating is not surrendering a point, it's usually setting up for future turns." Hold when the board or a hold-trigger battlefield makes it worth the risk.

### "The best decks post Vendetta banlist" (15 September 2026, ban day)

A tier list recorded an hour after the bans, so first impressions rather than testing. On Kha'Zix, he puts it around the top of B tier: it loses Stacked Deck's consistency but none of what makes it unique. The matchup reads:

| vs | Jun |
|---|---|
| **Azir** | **good matchup** |
| **Master Yi** (Proving Grounds) | **not so great** — and he rates Yi S tier |
| **Rengar** | even |
| A-tier decks generally | "some really strong matchups" |

**Why the ban helps Kha'Zix:** Kennen was gatekeeping the format, and "yellow decrees", the Decree spells, "were a big problem for Kha'Zix" and should now see less play. That is the same Decree of Unity he blamed in the Metafy update.

His read of the field, where it touches your matchups:
- **Jayce** is the best deck going into LA, but easily hated. "Every deck or almost every deck has cards that can hate" it, and **a turn-1 or turn-2 Ravenbloom Prefect could already be too much for a Jayce deck to handle.**
- **LeBlanc** rose to about A tier. It lost nothing, and with fewer Chaos decks around, Gust and Star-Crossed are less of a threat to it.
- **Akali** is hard to play, with **below-50% win rates** in his stats. It keeps doing well through a few excellent pilots, not broad success.
- **Rengar, Azir and Master Yi** are the three strongest decks, keeping each other in balance. Rengar vs Yi and Rengar vs Azir are both skill matchups.
- **Rek'Sai** may struggle in a Heron format, because the Heron decks are "extremely well equipped to hold" against it.
- **Vex** is B tier: "holding in general isn't the best strategy in Riftbound."

### "How to beat Irelia — Best-of-Kha'Zix live match commentary" (25 June 2026)

**Pre-ban, from the RQ Utrecht era**, on a list with Stacked Deck, Mister Root, Akshan and Arena's Greatest. Read it for the play patterns, not the cards. He calls Irelia "quite a good matchup" for Kha'Zix, then plays a best-of-three with his thinking out loud:

- **Sabotage first, to see what you are playing into.** He opens a key turn with Sabotage to learn whether the line is safe, and takes the card that swings fights most (Defiant Dance) over the counterspell.
- **Strip the Defy before the spell that matters.** Their plan is to keep Defy up to protect their Irelia from Star-Crossed. His answer is to make Sabotage, or a lesser spell, eat the Defy first, so Star-Crossed resolves. "If we bounce Irelia there, we are about to win the game."
- **Make them commit first.** Kha'Zix "represents six Might, which puts our opponent in the situation where they need to respond first", and a fight where you already lead on Might forces them to spend before you decide anything.
- **His own mistake, called on camera:** going into a Void Assault fight without buffing Kha'Zix first, against a possible second Defiant Dance. The second Dance came, and the fight was lost. **If a second trick would beat you, buff before the fight**: the legend cannot act mid-showdown, and a buff now is the only way to play around it.
- **The Vex lock closes games.** With their board cleared and Vex, Apathetic on a battlefield, anything they play is stunned and cannot move, so "they can't contest". Vex on each battlefield took him from 5 to 8 points with Irelia unable to answer.
- **Retreat rather than offer them a good turn.** With a fight looking bad, he walks Kha'Zix home and passes: "I don't want to offer my opponent a very good Irelia turn."
- **Battlefields change the matchup.** On Targon's Peak, which readies their runes after a conquest, he rates Irelia "slightly favoured", so he plans to win the games where it is not in play.
- **Sideboarding by play/draw, as his guide says.** On the draw he lowers his 2-drop count. On the play he wants all nine 2-drops and trims the top end.
